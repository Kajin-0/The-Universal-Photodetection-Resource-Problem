## Current practical thesis

The result stack now connects five standard measurement languages:

1. analog weak-signal detection: `1/NEP(f)^2` is the local Fisher weighting;
2. event detection: ideal timestamp FI reproduces the shot-noise limit, with jitter factor `|Phi_J|^2`;
3. detector memory: saturation curves and low-order recovery statistics need not determine timestamp information;
4. spectral support: seeded versus empty optical sidebands realize the survival-to-synthesis crossover;
5. Hamiltonian implementation: a textbook resonant beam-splitter model realizes `V_min=(1/2)Tr C` inside a fixed total-energy shell.

## WP01 — linear Gaussian bridge

`F_xx/T=F_yy/T=1/NEP(f)^2`, `Tr F/T=2/NEP(f)^2` under the locked peak-quadrature / one-sided-PSD convention.

## WP02 — ideal timestamps and jitter

Ideal fractional Poisson modulation: `Tr F/T=lambda_0`. Optical-power form exactly matches ideal shot-noise NEP. Independent jitter multiplies the spectrum by `|Phi_J(Omega)|^2`.

## WP03 — memory and dead time

Deterministic Type-II recovery at the saturation maximum is DC-information blind but retains every nonzero temporal mode. For arbitrary finite-mean iid recovery, all laws with mean `m` share `r=lambda exp(-lambda m)`, but `G_DC=0` at the common maximum iff recovery is deterministic. Exact equal-mean/equal-variance/equal-saturation recovery laws have different timestamp information.

## WP04 — optical survival-to-synthesis crossover

For seeded carrier/sideband baseline `rho_p`,

`R_lin^2=p(1-p)/[kappa^2(1-2p)^2]`,

`(R_lin^2/4)Tr F<=p`.

At the empty-sideband boundary,

`Delta P_s(0)=4kappa^2`, `Tr F<=Delta P_s(0)`,

with exact crossover

`lim_(p->0+)4p/R_lin^2=Delta P_s(0)`.

Ordinary ideal weak phase modulation saturates the bilateral curvature law with `Delta P_+=Delta P_-=1` and `Tr F=4` using a fixed phase-sensitive three-mode analyzer.

## WP05 — resonant exchange / unitary coupling

Use two resonant bosonic modes with

`H_0=hbar nu(N_C+N_S)`

and standard beam-splitter quadratures generated by `a_C^dagger a_S`.

In the fixed `N_tot=2` shell choose

`|L>=|2,0>`, `|M>=|1,1>`, `|U>=|0,2>`.

For

`U(x,y)=exp[-i g t(xB_x+yB_y)]`,

the tangent generators obey

`Var(K_x)=Var(K_y)=4(g t)^2`,

so

**`V_impl=8(g t)^2`.**

The endpoint populations satisfy

`Delta P_L=Delta P_U=8(g t)^2`,

hence

**`Tr C=16(g t)^2`,**

**`V_min=(1/2)Tr C=8(g t)^2`.**

The autonomous spectral action is

**`A_ex^(2)=hbar nu V_min=8 hbar nu(g t)^2`.**

The total bare-energy distribution remains exactly a delta function at `2hbar nu` for the entire family because the interaction commutes with `H_0`.

For a physical Hamiltonian quadrature `H_j` applied for fixed duration `t`,

**`V_impl=(t^2/hbar^2) sum_j Var(H_j)`.**

This is the standard-physics interpretation of the local coupling cost. It is not thermodynamic work, consumed RF energy, average interaction energy, operator norm, peak coupling, controller bandwidth, or a fixed-controller-spectrum optimum.

A practical test independently calibrates `g t` from exchange oscillations and independently measures the endpoint population Hessians, then compares

**`8(g t)^2=(1/2)[Delta P_L+Delta P_U]`.**
