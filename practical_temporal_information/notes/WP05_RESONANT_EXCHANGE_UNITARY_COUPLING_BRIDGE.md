# Part I — standard resonant beam-splitter model

## 1. Bare Hamiltonian and exchange generators

Let clock/controller mode `C` and signal mode `S` have the same angular frequency `nu`:

`H_0=hbar nu (N_C+N_S)`.

Define

`A=a_C^dagger a_S`.

The standard beam-splitter exchange quadratures are

`B_x=A+A^dagger`,

`B_y=-i(A-A^dagger)`.

Both are self-adjoint and commute with the total bare Hamiltonian:

`[H_0,B_x]=[H_0,B_y]=0`.

Thus every unitary generated by either quadrature remains inside fixed total-excitation / fixed total-bare-energy sectors.

A physical resonant beam-splitter Hamiltonian is the familiar

`H_BS=hbar g (e^{-i phi}A+e^{i phi}A^dagger)`,

whose phase selects a linear combination of `B_x` and `B_y`.

## 2. Fixed-shell three-state benchmark

Work in the `N_C+N_S=2` manifold and choose

`|L>=|2,0>`,

`|M>=|1,1>`,

`|U>=|0,2>`.

All three states have exactly the same total bare energy

`E_tot=2 hbar nu`.

The exchange matrix element is

`a_C^dagger a_S |1,1>=sqrt(2)|2,0>`,

`a_C a_S^dagger |1,1>=sqrt(2)|0,2>`.

Therefore

`B_x|M>=sqrt(2)(|L>+|U>)`,

`B_y|M>=-i sqrt(2)(|L>-|U>)`.

This is the standard spin-1 / two-boson beam-splitter representation of the abstract three-state fixed-shell benchmark used in the PRA supplement.

## 3. Parameterized interaction and tangent generators

Take a fixed interaction duration `t` and define two dimensionless local coupling quadratures `x,y` through

`U(x,y)=exp[-i g t (x B_x+y B_y)]`.

The PRA tangent generators are therefore

`K_x=g t B_x`,

`K_y=g t B_y`.

The parameters `x,y` are dimensionless relative coupling quadratures. The experimentally calibrated dimensionless interaction angle is `g t`.

At the baseline `|M>`,

`<K_x>=<K_y>=0`.

Because

`||B_x|M>||^2=||B_y|M>||^2=4`,

one has

**`Var_M(K_x)=4(g t)^2`,**

**`Var_M(K_y)=4(g t)^2`.**

Hence

**`V_impl=8(g t)^2`.**

This is a dimensionless state-weighted quadratic generator cost. It is determined by the calibrated exchange angle and baseline occupation, not by an energy-consumption integral.

# Part II — endpoint curvature gives the same number

## 4. Small-coupling endpoint populations

To first order in `x,y`,

`-i(g t)(xB_x+yB_y)|M>`

places amplitudes in both endpoints. Their magnitudes are

`|amp_L|^2=2(g t)^2(x^2+y^2)+O(r^4)`,

`|amp_U|^2=2(g t)^2(x^2+y^2)+O(r^4)`,

where `r^2=x^2+y^2`.

Therefore

`P_L=2(g t)^2 r^2+O(r^4)`,

`P_U=2(g t)^2 r^2+O(r^4)`.

With `Delta=partial_x^2+partial_y^2`,

**`Delta P_L(0)=8(g t)^2`,**

**`Delta P_U(0)=8(g t)^2`.**

The prescribed kernel Hessian contraction is supported on the two empty endpoints and has trace

**`Tr C=Delta(P_L+P_U)=16(g t)^2`.**

Thus this standard resonant exchange satisfies

**`V_impl=(1/2)Tr C=8(g t)^2`.**

Because the baseline support-block expectation/variance contribution vanishes, the model saturates the universal lower bound and therefore realizes the exact minimum:

**`V_min=8(g t)^2`.**

## 5. Mapping to the PRA three-state notation

The PRA supplement uses a constant `c` such that

`Var(K_x)=Var(K_y)=c^2/2`,

`V_impl=c^2`,

and endpoint action is `hbar nu c^2`.

The beam-splitter model gives the exact identification

**`c^2=8(g t)^2`.**

Equivalently,

`c=2sqrt(2) g t`

for positive calibration convention.

This makes the abstract benchmark directly recognizable as the two-boson resonant beam-splitter manifold.

# Part III — autonomous spectral action in standard quantities

## 6. Exact synthesis action

For a clean single-gap autonomous exchange, the verified PRA normalization is

`A_ex^(2)=(hbar nu/2)Tr C=hbar nu V_min`.

Using the beam-splitter result,

**`A_ex^(2)=8 hbar nu (g t)^2`.**

Equivalently, in the PRA `c` normalization,

`A_ex^(2)=hbar nu c^2`.

The local energy quantum `hbar nu` is ordinary: every exchange raises one resonant mode by one quantum while lowering the other by one quantum.

## 7. QFI cross-check at minimum curvature

The local pure-state derivatives have squared norm

`||partial_x psi||^2=||partial_y psi||^2=4(g t)^2`,

and are orthogonal in the relevant real sense.

Therefore

`H_xx=H_yy=16(g t)^2`,

so

**`Tr H^SLD=32(g t)^2`.**

The first-order/minimum-curvature identity becomes

`V_min=(1/4)Tr H^SLD=8(g t)^2`,

and the spectral action becomes

**`A_ex^(2)=(hbar nu/4)Tr H^SLD=8 hbar nu(g t)^2`.**

Thus the standard beam-splitter model simultaneously saturates the first-order QFI bridge, the prescribed-curvature unitary-coupling minimum, and the autonomous spectral action identity.

# Part IV — why this is not work or consumed power

## 8. Total bare energy is exactly fixed

The full family remains in the `N_C+N_S=2` eigenspace because

`[H_0,U(x,y)]=0`.

Therefore for every `x,y`,

**`<H_0>=2 hbar nu`,**

**`Var(H_0)=0`.**

The entire total bare-energy distribution is a delta function and never changes.

Yet

`V_min=8(g t)^2>0`

for nonzero coupling.

This is the cleanest standard-physics explanation of the PRA statement:

> nonzero coupling required to synthesize relational information does not imply net energy consumption or global energy asymmetry.

The resource is transition-generating interaction strength as sampled by the baseline state.

## 9. Relation to interaction-Hamiltonian variance

For a fixed interaction duration `t`, a physical Hamiltonian quadrature is

`H_x=hbar g B_x`,

`H_y=hbar g B_y`.

Since

`K_j=(t/hbar)H_j`,

one has generally

**`V_impl=(t^2/hbar^2) sum_j Var(H_j)`.**

For the `|1,1>` beam-splitter baseline,

`Var(H_x)=Var(H_y)=4 hbar^2 g^2`,

which reproduces

`V_impl=8(g t)^2`.

This is perhaps the most conventional interpretation of the PRA cost: it is the time-integrated parameter-generator variance for the local interaction family.

It is **not**:

- `int P(t)dt` consumed electrical energy;
- thermodynamic work;
- average interaction energy `<H_int>`;
- the operator norm `||H_int||`;
- maximum instantaneous coupling;
- controller bandwidth;
- controller dimension;
- the optimum under an externally fixed ancillary/controller spectrum.

Indeed, in this benchmark `<H_x>=<H_y>=0` while their variances are nonzero.

# Part V — practical calibration/falsification protocol

## 10. Independent calibration routes

The equality can be tested without estimating every abstract operator.

### Route A — coupling calibration

1. calibrate resonant exchange rate `g` from ordinary Rabi/beam-splitter oscillations or mode splitting;
2. set the interaction duration `t`;
3. prepare the fixed-shell baseline `|1,1>`;
4. infer

`V_impl=8(g t)^2`.

### Route B — curvature calibration

Independently vary small coupling quadratures `x,y` and measure probabilities of endpoints `|2,0>` and `|0,2>`.

Fit

`P_L=alpha_L(x^2+y^2)+...`,

`P_U=alpha_U(x^2+y^2)+...`.

The ideal beam-splitter model predicts

`alpha_L=alpha_U=2(g t)^2`,

or

`Delta P_L=Delta P_U=8(g t)^2`.

Then

`(1/2)Tr C=(1/2)[Delta P_L+Delta P_U]`.

### Prediction

**`8(g t)^2 = (1/2)[Delta P_L+Delta P_U]`.**

This is the simplest experimental equality corresponding to `V_min=(1/2)Tr C` in this model.

## 11. Falsification test E — fixed-shell implementation identity

Required checks:

1. verify the interaction is resonant and number conserving to the required accuracy;
2. independently calibrate `g t`;
3. independently measure endpoint population Hessians;
4. verify leakage outside the `N_tot=2` manifold is negligible or include it in the model;
5. compare the two sides of the equality above.

A statistically significant calibrated failure of

`(1/2)Tr C=8(g t)^2`

would falsify the ideal resonant beam-splitter implementation model. If the full unitary-dilation assumptions and parameter normalization are independently verified, a violation below the theorem lower bound would challenge the theorem application itself.

The practical distinction is important: most real deviations will first indicate Hamiltonian/model imperfections, not a fundamental theorem violation.

## 12. Optional Fisher-information cross-check

A phase-sensitive measurement on the three fixed-shell states can estimate the two local coupling quadratures. The ideal model predicts

`Tr H^SLD=32(g t)^2`

and an attainable compatible Fisher trace equal to the QFI trace with a suitable analyzer.

Then one may additionally test

`V_min=(1/4)Tr H^SLD`

at the minimum-curvature point.

This is optional for the implementation-cost test because endpoint curvature plus independently calibrated coupling already tests the main equality.

# Part VI — relation to accessible platforms

## 13. Physical interpretations

The Hamiltonian

`H_BS=hbar g(a_C^dagger a_S+a_C a_S^dagger)`

is standard for:

- evanescently coupled resonators in a rotating-frame/resonant description;
- microwave or optical beam-splitter interactions;
- frequency conversion after the appropriate resonant mode definition;
- state-swap / excitation-exchange couplings.

The exact three-state benchmark uses the two-excitation manifold `|2,0>,|1,1>,|0,2>`.

For unequal bare mode frequencies, an exactly energy-conserving frequency converter requires the pump/controller energy to be included explicitly. Do not silently use a classical undepleted pump and still call the reduced two-mode Hamiltonian globally energy conserving. That more detailed controller model is unnecessary for the present benchmark.
