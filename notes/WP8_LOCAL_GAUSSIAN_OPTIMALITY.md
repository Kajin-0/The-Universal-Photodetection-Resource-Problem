# WP8 — Local Gaussian optimality near thermal equilibrium

**Date:** 2026-08-20

## Purpose

The exact non-Gaussian parity counterexample proves that Gaussian pointers are not globally optimal at finite free energy. This note establishes the complementary local result:

> **For sufficiently small nonequilibrium free-energy budget, Gaussian perturbations are asymptotically optimal to leading order over all pointer states.**

Together with the finite-budget parity counterexample, this implies a genuine resource-driven Gaussian-to-non-Gaussian crossover in the globally optimal detector pointer.

---

## 1. Thermal reference and displacement QFI

Let

\[
\tau_\vartheta=(1-e^{-\vartheta})e^{-\vartheta N},
\qquad
\vartheta=\beta\hbar\omega>0.
\]

For translation generated by `P`, define

\[
J_X(\rho)
=F_Q[e^{-i\theta P}\rho e^{i\theta P};\theta]_{\theta=0}.
\]

The thermal-state quadrature variance is

\[
V_X
=\frac12\coth\frac\vartheta2.
\]

Therefore its SLD is

\[
L_\beta
=\frac{X}{V_X}
=2\tanh\frac\vartheta2\,X.
\]

Define

\[
t\equiv\tanh\frac\vartheta2.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{J_\beta=2t.}
\]

---

## 2. Variational representation of SLD QFI

For a differentiable state family with tangent `dot rho`, SLD QFI has the variational representation

\[
F_Q(\rho)
=\sup_{L=L^\dagger}
\left[
2\operatorname{Tr}(\dot\rho L)
-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho L^2)
\right].
\]

For the unitary translation tangent

\[
\dot\rho=-i[P,\rho],
\]

this becomes

\[
J_X(\rho)
=\sup_L\operatorname{Tr}[\rho K_L],
\]

with

\[
K_L
=2i[P,L]-L^2.
\]

At the faithful thermal state the optimizing SLD is unique and equals `L_beta`. Therefore the envelope theorem gives the first variation

\[
\delta J_X
=\operatorname{Tr}(\delta\rho K_\beta),
\]

where

\[
K_\beta
=2i[P,L_\beta]-L_\beta^2.
\]

Using `i[P,X]=1`,

\[
\boxed{
K_\beta
=4t-4t^2X^2.
}
\]

Up to an irrelevant scalar constant, the QFI gradient is therefore proportional to `-X^2`.

**Status:** PROVED.

## 3. Relative entropy supplies the BKM local metric

For a small perturbation around a faithful state,

\[
\rho=\tau_\vartheta+\epsilon\delta\rho,
\qquad
\operatorname{Tr}\delta\rho=0,
\]

the relative entropy has the quadratic expansion

\[
D(\rho\Vert\tau_\vartheta)
=\frac{\epsilon^2}{2}
\langle\delta\rho,\delta\rho\rangle_{\rm BKM}
+O(\epsilon^3),
\]

where the Bogoliubov-Kubo-Mori metric is the Hessian of quantum relative entropy.

Equivalently, for an exponential tilt

\[
\rho_\lambda
=\frac{e^{\log\tau_\vartheta+\lambda K_\beta}}
{\operatorname{Tr}e^{\log\tau_\vartheta+\lambda K_\beta}},
\]

the relative entropy and expectation obey

\[
D(\rho_\lambda\Vert\tau)
=\frac{\lambda^2}{2}\mathcal V_{\rm KM}(K_\beta)
+O(\lambda^3),
\]

\[
\operatorname{Tr}[\rho_\lambda K_\beta]
-\operatorname{Tr}[\tau K_\beta]
=\lambda\mathcal V_{\rm KM}(K_\beta)
+O(\lambda^2),
\]

where

\[
\mathcal V_{\rm KM}(K)
=\int_0^1du\,
\operatorname{Tr}[\tau^u\widetilde K\tau^{1-u}\widetilde K]
\]

and `Ktilde=K-<K>_tau`.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the BKM metric shows that this exponential-tilt direction is the unique steepest-ascent direction for the linearized QFI under a small relative-entropy budget.

## 4. The locally optimal tilt is Gaussian

Because

\[
\log\tau_\vartheta
=\text{const}
-\frac\vartheta2(X^2+P^2)
\]

and

\[
K_\beta
=\text{const}-4t^2X^2,
\]

the optimal exponential tilt

\[
\rho_\lambda
\propto
\exp[\log\tau+\lambda K_\beta]
\]

is a quadratic Gibbs state: a squeezed thermal Gaussian state.

Therefore Gaussian states attain the globally optimal first-order QFI improvement for `D_0 -> 0`.

**Status:** PROVED asymptotic local optimality over arbitrary states.

## 5. Explicit small-budget expansion

The Kubo-Mori variance of `X^2` can be evaluated from the quadratic partition function

\[
Z_X(\eta)
=\operatorname{Tr}
\exp(\log\tau_\vartheta+\eta X^2).
\]

Define

\[
\mu(\eta)=\sqrt{\vartheta(\vartheta-2\eta)}.
\]

Then

\[
Z_X(\eta)
=(1-e^{-\vartheta})
\frac{e^{(\vartheta-\mu)/2}}
{1-e^{-\mu}}.
\]

Hence

\[
\mathcal V_{\rm KM}(X^2)
=\left.\frac{d^2}{d\eta^2}\ln Z_X(\eta)\right|_{\eta=0}
\]

which gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_{\rm KM}(X^2)
=\frac{1}{2\vartheta}
\coth\frac\vartheta2
+\frac14\operatorname{csch}^2\frac\vartheta2.
}
\]

Since the centered QFI gradient is `-4t^2(X^2-<X^2>)`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal V_{\rm KM}(K_\beta)
=16t^4\mathcal V_{\rm KM}(X^2)
=\frac{8t^3}{\vartheta}
+4t^2(1-t^2).
}
\]

Therefore the exact leading small-free-energy frontier is

\[
\boxed{
J_F^{\max}(D_0,\vartheta)
=2t
+\sqrt{
2\left[
\frac{8t^3}{\vartheta}
+4t^2(1-t^2)
\right]D_0
}
+O(D_0).
}
\]

A squeezed thermal Gaussian state attains this expression through the displayed order.

## 6. Consequence: a Gaussian-to-non-Gaussian crossover must occur

`WP8_NON_GAUSSIAN_PARITY_COUNTEREXAMPLE.md` gives the exact finite-resource point

\[
\vartheta=\ln2,
\qquad
D_0=\ln(3/2),
\]

where an even-conditioned thermal non-Gaussian state has

\[
J_X=14/3,
\]

while every Gaussian state at the same budget obeys

\[
J_X^{\rm Gaussian}<3.783.
\]

But the present theorem proves that Gaussian states are optimal as `D_0 -> 0`.

By continuity of the relevant achievable frontiers, at this temperature there must therefore be at least one finite resource scale where the globally optimal pointer changes character from Gaussian to non-Gaussian.

Schematically,

\[
\boxed{
\text{near equilibrium: Gaussian optimum}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{finite resource: non-Gaussian parity advantage}.
}
\]

This is not merely a numerical crossover; the two endpoint statements are analytic.

## 7. Research significance

The quantum apparatus-preparation problem now has three rigorous regimes:

1. **Small free energy:** Gaussian squeezed-thermal perturbations are asymptotically globally optimal.
2. **Finite free energy:** Gaussian global optimality fails; parity-protected non-Gaussian states can do strictly better.
3. **Large free energy:** the exact even-parity exponential family is asymptotically globally optimal to leading relative order.

The remaining unsolved region is the exact finite-budget interpolation, especially the optimal treatment of both parity sectors below and around the crossover.
