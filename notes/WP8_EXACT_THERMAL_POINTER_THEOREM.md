# WP8 — Exact thermal-pointer temperature/coupling/information theorem

**Date:** 2026-08-20

## Purpose

The general free-energy problem requires optimization over detector preparation resources. The equilibrium special case `Delta F=0` is much cleaner and yields an exact temperature-dependent QFI-transfer theorem for coherent optical displacement under passive linear coupling.

---

## 1. Thermal pointer QFI

Let the detector pointer be a harmonic mode of frequency `omega_D` initially in

\[
\tau_\beta
=(1-e^{-\vartheta})e^{-\vartheta N},
\qquad
\vartheta=\beta\hbar\omega_D.
\]

Its quadrature variance is

\[
V_X=\frac12\coth\frac\vartheta2.
\]

For a unit displacement generated by `P`, the SLD QFI is

\[
\boxed{
J_\beta
=\frac1{V_X}
=2\tanh\frac\vartheta2.
}
\]

Define

\[
t_\beta\equiv\tanh(\vartheta/2).
\]

---

## 2. Passive optical-to-pointer transfer

For a coherent optical displacement source mixed into the thermal detector pointer with source-to-detector probability `tau`, the directional SLD-Stam theorem gives

\[
\frac{F_{\rm elec}}{F_{\rm in}}
\le
\frac{\tau J_\beta}
{2(1-\tau)+\tau J_\beta}.
\]

Substituting `J_beta=2 t_beta`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_{\rm elec}}{F_{\rm in}}
\le
\frac{\tau t_\beta}
{1-\tau+\tau t_\beta}.
}
\]

For the Gaussian thermal pointer and optimal quadrature measurement, this inequality is saturated. Thus it is an **exact optimum** within the passive-linear single-effective-mode model.

**Status:** PROVED and saturable.

---

## 3. Interaction-action form

The passive single-particle transfer theorem gives

\[
\tau\le\sin^2\Gamma,
\qquad
\Gamma=\int_0^t\|V(s)\|_2ds,
\]

for `0<=Gamma<=pi/2`.

Because the information fraction is monotone in `tau`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_{\rm elec}}{F_{\rm in}}
\le
\frac{t_\beta\sin^2\Gamma}
{\cos^2\Gamma+t_\beta\sin^2\Gamma}.
}
\]

This is a direct finite-temperature interaction-action information-transfer theorem.

---

## 4. Necessary action for target transfer fraction

To achieve

\[
F_{\rm elec}/F_{\rm in}\ge q,
\qquad 0<q<1,
\]

one must have

\[
\frac{\tau}{1-\tau}
\ge
\frac{q}{t_\beta(1-q)}.
\]

Since `tau<=sin^2 Gamma`,

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma
\ge
\arctan
\sqrt{
\frac{q}
{t_\beta(1-q)}
}.
}
\]

At zero temperature, `t_beta -> 1` and this reduces to

\[
\Gamma\ge\arcsin\sqrt q.
\]

At high temperature,

\[
t_\beta
\simeq\frac{\hbar\omega_D}{2k_BT},
\]

so

\[
\boxed{
\tan^2\Gamma
\gtrsim
\frac{2k_BT}{\hbar\omega_D}
\frac{q}{1-q}.
}
\]

Thus a weakly coupled equilibrium pointer becomes increasingly incapable of transferring optical displacement information as `k_B T / hbar omega_D` grows.

---

## 5. Interpretation

This theorem isolates the role of temperature without hidden nonequilibrium apparatus preparation:

- `T` fixes the pointer's equilibrium metrological noise;
- `Gamma` quantifies the time-integrated optical-to-pointer coupling action;
- their combination bounds the fraction of incident coherent-state QFI that can reach the electrical record.

The theorem does **not** apply unchanged once the detector is pre-squeezed, pumped, inverted, or otherwise prepared out of equilibrium. WP7/WP8 prove that such preloaded resources can overcome the equilibrium thermal penalty and must therefore be budgeted explicitly.

---

This is a quantum analogue of the classical WP3/WP4 logic:

\[
\text{temperature alone does not bound speed if hidden resources are allowed,}
\]

but

\[
\boxed{
\text{equilibrium apparatus}
+
\text{bounded interaction action}
\Rightarrow
\text{finite QFI-transfer speed ceiling}.
}
\]
