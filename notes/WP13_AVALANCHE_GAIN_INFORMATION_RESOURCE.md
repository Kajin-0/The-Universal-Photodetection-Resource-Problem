# WP13 — Avalanche gain as an information/resource layer

**Date:** 2026-08-20

## Purpose

Stress-test active internal gain. Conventional APDs improve electrical signal amplitude and can overcome downstream electronics noise, but avalanche multiplication is stochastic and produces excess noise.

UPRP asks the sharper question: **can gain create optical information, and what resources/noise terms determine when gain is useful?**

Main conclusions:

1. deterministic invertible gain cannot increase Fisher information; it only rescales the record;
2. stochastic multiplication is a parameter-independent downstream channel, so data processing forbids it from exceeding the primary photoelectron FI;
3. in the high-count linear-Gaussian regime, gain trades downstream electronics noise against avalanche excess noise;
4. the optimal gain follows an explicit equation under the McIntyre excess-noise model;
5. physical gain also requires bias/pump free energy and finite multiplication-region dynamics, which belong on the resource side even though they do not appear in the data-processing inequality.

---

# 1. Exact data-processing theorem

Let `N` denote the complete primary photoelectron record generated by the optical parameter `theta`.

Let avalanche multiplication and downstream noiseless charge collection be a parameter-independent stochastic channel

\[
N\to Y.
\]

Classical Fisher information obeys data processing:

\[
\boxed{F_Y(\theta)\le F_N(\theta).}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\eta_I^{\rm after\ gain}\le\eta_I^{\rm primary}.}
\]

No avalanche architecture can create information about the optical parameter that was absent from the primary carrier record.

**Status:** PROVED by classical FI data processing.

---

# 2. Deterministic gain preserves information

If

\[
Y=M N,
\qquad M\ne0
\]

with perfectly known deterministic gain and exact continuous readout, the transformation is invertible:

\[
N=Y/M.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{F_Y=F_N.}
\]

So

\[
\boxed{\text{deterministic gain is information-invariant}.}
\]

This is the active-device analogue of the deterministic LTI-filter invariance in WP12.

Physical gain may still be highly useful because it raises the electrical signal above **later** noise/quantization floors.

---

# 3. Compound-Poisson avalanche model

Let primary events in one estimation interval be

\[
N\sim{\rm Poisson}(\lambda).
\]

Each primary carrier independently generates random multiplication `M_i` with

\[
\bar M=\mathbb E[M_i],
\]

and define excess-noise factor

\[
\boxed{
F_M=\frac{\mathbb E[M_i^2]}{\bar M^2}\ge1.
}
\]

The total multiplied charge in electron units is

\[
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{N}M_i.
\]

Compound-Poisson moments are

\[
\boxed{\mathbb E[Q]=\lambda\bar M,}
\]

\[
\boxed{{\rm Var}(Q)=\lambda\mathbb E[M_i^2]
=\lambda\bar M^2F_M.}
\]

The exact likelihood contains more information than these two moments, but by data processing it never exceeds the primary Poisson FI.

---

# 4. Linear-Gaussian information approximation

Consider weak fractional modulation of the primary mean,

\[
\lambda(\theta)=\lambda_0(1+\theta),
\]

so the primary Poisson FI at `theta=0` is

\[
F_{\rm in}=\lambda_0.
\]

In the high-count/small-signal Gaussian approximation, treat the baseline multiplied-current noise as stationary with variance

\[
V_{\rm mult}
=\lambda_0\bar M^2F_M.
\]

Add independent downstream electronics noise variance

\[
\sigma_e^2.
\]

The mean derivative is

\[
\partial_\theta\mathbb E[Q]
=\lambda_0\bar M.
\]

Therefore the local mean-channel FI is

\[
F_{\rm out}
\simeq
\frac{\lambda_0^2\bar M^2}
{\lambda_0\bar M^2F_M+\sigma_e^2}.
\]

Normalizing by primary Poisson FI gives

\[
\boxed{
\eta_I^{\rm G}
\simeq
\frac{1}
{F_M+\sigma_e^2/(\lambda_0\bar M^2)}.
}
\]

This formula should be used only in the high-count/linear-Gaussian regime. The exact compound-Poisson FI remains bounded by 1 through data processing.

---

# 5. Interpretation

The denominator separates two effects:

\[
\boxed{
\text{input-referred information penalty}
=
\text{avalanche excess noise}
+
\text{downstream electronics noise}/\bar M^2.
}
\]

Thus gain is useful only because it reduces the second term.

If downstream noise vanishes,

\[
\boxed{
\eta_I^{\rm G}\to1/F_M.
}
\]

For deterministic multiplication `F_M=1`, the primary information is preserved.

For stochastic APD multiplication `F_M>1`, internal gain sacrifices some primary information even before downstream electronics are considered.

---

# 6. McIntyre excess-noise model

For electron-initiated multiplication in the local McIntyre model,

\[
\boxed{
F(M)
=kM+(1-k)\left(2-\frac1M\right),
}
\]

where `k` is the hole/electron ionization-coefficient ratio in the conventional notation.

This is established APD theory and not novel.

Let downstream-noise parameter in primary-electron-count units be

\[
\boxed{A=\sigma_e^2/\lambda_0.}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\eta_I(M)
\simeq
\frac1{
F(M)+A/M^2
}.
}
\]

Maximizing information is equivalent to minimizing

\[
D(M)=F(M)+A/M^2.
\]

---

# 7. Exact optimal-gain equation

Differentiate:

\[
D'(M)
=k+\frac{1-k}{M^2}-\frac{2A}{M^3}.
\]

An interior optimum satisfies

\[
\boxed{
kM^3+(1-k)M-2A=0.}
\]

For `k>=0`, the left side is strictly increasing for `M>0`, so there is at most one positive interior root.

The physical optimum is

\[
\boxed{
M_{\rm opt}=\max[1,M_*]
}
\]

where `M_*` is the positive cubic root when it exceeds unity.

Special single-carrier limit `k=0`:

\[
\boxed{M_*=2A.}
\]

Thus high electronics noise favors larger multiplication, while avalanche excess noise limits the useful gain.

**Status:** PROVED within the stated linear-Gaussian + McIntyre model.

---

# 8. High-gain behavior

For `k>0`,

\[
F(M)\sim kM
\]

at high gain, so

\[
\eta_I(M)\sim1/(kM)\to0
\]

once downstream electronics noise is already negligible.

Thus arbitrarily large gain is not information-optimal in the conventional stochastic-avalanche model.

For `k=0`,

\[
F(M)\to2,
\]

so even ideal one-carrier multiplication approaches a high-gain Gaussian information fraction near `1/2` rather than unity when the multiplication itself remains stochastic.

Modern dead-space/low-noise avalanche structures can deviate substantially from the local McIntyre model and must be treated with their actual multiplication statistics.

---

# 9. Standard APD noise anchor

APD shot-noise expressions use

\[
S_I\propto M^2F(M)
\]

and modern APD literature continues to characterize avalanche sensitivity through multiplication gain plus excess-noise factor.

Recent high-speed Si/Ge APD work, for example, uses

\[
I_{\rm shot}^2
=2qM^2(I_{\rm photo}+I_{\rm dark})F(M)\Delta f
\]

and the standard McIntyre-type `F(M)` expression.

Do not claim these formulas as new.

---

# 10. Bias/pump free energy is an independent resource

The statistical information theorem says gain cannot create source information. It does **not** say gain is free.

Avalanche multiplication requires a reverse-bias electric field and converts electrical bias energy into additional carrier energy/charge.

Therefore a resource-complete active-detector theorem should include at least:

- bias voltage/electric-field resource;
- multiplication-region length/time;
- impact-ionization statistics/excess noise;
- dissipated/bias power or free-energy throughput;
- downstream electronics noise that the gain is intended to overcome.

A detector with arbitrarily large deterministic noiseless gain would preserve information mathematically but would still require unbounded or otherwise explicit physical amplification resources.

---

# 11. Relation to UPRP hidden-resource logic

Gain introduces another no-go pair:

\[
\boxed{
\text{large electrical amplitude gain alone}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{large optical information gain}.
}
\]

The useful active resource is instead

\[
\boxed{
\text{gain}
+\text{multiplication statistics}
+\text{bias/free-energy cost}
+\text{downstream noise floor}.
}
\]

This parallels WP12: attenuation/gain only matters for information relative to where noise and coarse graining enter.
