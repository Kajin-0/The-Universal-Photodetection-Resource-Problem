# WP8 — What resource repairs the UV coherence loophole?

**Date:** 2026-08-20

## Purpose

`WP8_UV_NON_GAUSSIAN_INSTABILITY.md` proves that finite nonequilibrium free energy does not make the Gaussian harmonic-pointer optimum exact: arbitrarily high-Fock, vanishing-amplitude coherences can increase displacement QFI at the same resource budget.

This note asks what additional physical resource can actually remove that loophole.

The main result is negative and useful:

> **No resource constraint that depends only on the detector's diagonal energy distribution or its energy moments can remove the UV coherence instability.**

A coherence-/support-sensitive microscopic resource is necessary.

---

# 1. Parity structure of the instability

The harmonic pointer Hamiltonian is

\[
H_D=\hbar\omega_D(N+1/2).
\]

It commutes with parity

\[
\Pi=(-1)^N.
\]

The centered instability perturbations constructed in `WP8_UV_NON_GAUSSIAN_INSTABILITY.md` are parity odd:

\[
\Pi\,\delta\rho\,\Pi=-\delta\rho.
\]

Every spectral function of the Hamiltonian is parity even:

\[
[f(H_D),\Pi]=0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Tr}[\delta\rho\,f(H_D)]=0
}
\]

for every well-defined scalar function `f`.

In particular,

\[
\boxed{
\delta\langle H_D^k\rangle=0
\qquad(k=1,2,3,\ldots).
}
\]

The off-diagonal perturbation changes no energy-level population at all. Thus all diagonal moments are unchanged along the linear perturbation.

**Status:** PROVED.

---

# 2. Consequence: adding more energy moments does not cure the problem

The following repairs are insufficient by themselves:

- fixed mean energy;
- fixed energy variance;
- fixed finite collection of energy moments;
- even the complete energy probability distribution, if off-diagonal coherence is otherwise unrestricted.

The instability lives in coherence between opposite-parity sectors, which none of these diagonal resources can see.

This is stronger than the statement that mean energy is a loose resource. The missing variable is qualitatively different from an energy-population budget.

---

# 3. Standard energetic-coherence QFI is also not automatically sufficient in an ideal harmonic ladder

Quantum thermodynamics distinguishes work/free-energy resources from **energetic coherence/asymmetry** resources. Marvian (PRL 129, 190502, 2022) shows that SLD QFI with respect to time translations generated by the Hamiltonian determines an asymptotic energetic-coherence formation cost.

It is therefore natural to ask whether adding

\[
F_H(\rho)=F_Q(\rho,H_D)
\]

regularizes the WP8 UV instability.

For the simplest thermal baseline, take the adjacent coherence

\[
B_n=(|n\rangle\langle n+1|+\mathrm{h.c.})/\sqrt2.
\]

Because adjacent harmonic levels always have the same energy gap, the energetic-coherence QFI Hessian scales as

\[
\delta^2F_H[B_n]
\propto\frac1{r_n+r_{n+1}},
\]

with no growing factor of `n` from the energy gap.

By contrast the displacement-QFI Hessian contains the harmonic-oscillator matrix element

\[
|\langle n|P|n+1\rangle|^2=(n+1)/2,
\]

and grows linearly faster.

For the thermal state `q=e^{-vartheta}`, the exact large-`n` ratio is

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\delta^2J_X[B_n]}
{\delta^2F_N[B_n]}
\sim
2\frac{1+q+q^2}{1+q^2}\,n,
}
\]

where `F_N` uses the dimensionless Hamiltonian generator `N`.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\delta^2J_X/\delta^2F_N\to\infty.
}
\]

So a scalar bound on the ordinary time-translation QFI/asymmetry resource does **not** by itself impose a uniform bound on displacement metrological curvature in an exactly harmonic infinite ladder.

**Status:** PROVED for the thermal adjacent-coherence family.

This does not contradict Marvian's thermodynamic coherence-cost result. It says that the amount of **time-translation asymmetry** is not the same microscopic resource as the high-level matrix elements of the displacement generator.

# 4. Physical origin

The ideal harmonic oscillator has two simultaneous features:

1. level spacing remains constant for arbitrarily large `n`;
2. displacement-generator matrix elements grow without bound:

\[
|P_{n,n+1}|\sim\sqrt n.
\]

Thus one can move a fixed amount of energetic coherence to higher excitation number without increasing the time-translation frequency of that coherence, while gaining a larger displacement matrix element.

This is an ultraviolet pathology of the ideal oscillator model, not a generic statement about finite physical detectors.

---

# 5. Resources that can repair the loophole

A valid repair must control the off-diagonal high-energy structure or the unbounded signal-generator matrix elements.

Physically credible candidates include:

## A. Hard excitation cutoff / finite Hilbert dimension

\[
N\le N_{\max}.
\]

Then `P` becomes bounded and the Hessian ratio has a finite maximum. This is the cleanest mathematical repair.

## B. Microscopic saturation / anharmonicity

Real detector pointer modes eventually depart from an ideal harmonic ladder. If transition matrix elements cease growing as `sqrt(n)`, or level spacings/gaps grow, the UV construction can fail.

## C. Bound on the signal-generator norm or graph-weighted transition strength

A resource such as

\[
\|P\|_{\rm eff}
\]

or an energy-resolved bound on

\[
|P_{mn}|^2
\]

directly controls the matrix elements responsible for the instability.

This is mathematically effective but must not merely restate the detector bandwidth being bounded.

## D. Preparation-channel restrictions

A bounded-strength, bounded-bandwidth preparation Hamiltonian over finite preparation time may make the UV coherences inaccessible even if the abstract final-state free energy is small.

This is operationally attractive because it treats the hidden resource as **state-preparation complexity**, not as another ad hoc static moment.

## E. Microscopic oscillator-strength/sum-rule resource

For a detector pointer derived from real matter rather than an abstract bosonic mode, transition-matrix elements may be constrained by matter sum rules and finite participating degrees of freedom. This is conceptually parallel to WP5's optical frontend closure and deserves a dedicated mapping.

# 6. Finite-cutoff scaling

For the adjacent-coherence direction, the exact Hessian ratio is

\[
R_n
=\alpha(\mu,\vartheta)n+O(1),
\]

where

\[
\alpha(\mu,\vartheta)
=
\frac{8(1-e^{-\mu})}{\vartheta}
\frac{1+e^{-\mu}+e^{-2\mu}}
{(1+e^{-\mu})(1+e^{-2\mu})}.
\]

With a hard maximum excitation `N_max`, this particular instability is absent whenever the Gaussian multiplier obeys approximately

\[
\lambda_G
\gtrsim
\alpha N_{\max}.
\]

Since near equilibrium

\[
\lambda_G\sim c_G/(2\sqrt{D_0}),
\]

a cutoff generates a finite Gaussian-regular regime with characteristic scaling

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm reg}
\sim
\frac{c_G^2}{4\alpha^2N_{\max}^2}
}
\]

up to order-unity constants and other possible finite-dimensional Hessian modes.

Thus the exact Gaussian neighborhood shrinks as the available excitation manifold grows.

This is a scaling estimate from the proved adjacent-coherence mode, not yet a complete finite-dimensional stability theorem.

# 7. Resource-completeness interpretation

The quantum resource hierarchy has now become more explicit:

\[
\boxed{
\text{free energy / work}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{bounded apparatus metrological structure}
}
\]

and even

\[
\boxed{
\text{free energy + diagonal energy moments}
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\text{UV-regular displacement QFI}.
}
\]

The detector specification must additionally contain a microscopic resource controlling the **accessible high-energy coherence and/or the signal-generator matrix elements**.

This is closely analogous to the classical WP4 result where stationary thermodynamic costs failed to control an absolute hidden transition-rate scale.
