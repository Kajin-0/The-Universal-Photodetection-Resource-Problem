## 1. Structured globally stationary relative-time experiment

Let clock `C` and signal `S` have equally spaced local excitation generators `N_C,N_S>=0` with common quantum `hbar omega0`.

Restrict the joint apparatus to a fixed total-excitation shell

`N_C+N_S=L`.

A convenient basis is

`|n>_rel := |L-n>_C tensor |n>_S`, `n=0,...,L`.

Every state supported in this shell is exactly stationary under the total Hamiltonian

`H_tot=hbar omega0(N_C+N_S)`

up to an irrelevant global phase.

Relative phase translations may be generated by `N_S`; inside the fixed-total shell this is equivalent, up to global phase, to the opposite translation of the clock.

Take a pure seed

`|Psi>=sum_(n=0)^L sqrt(q_n) |n>_rel`

and randomize its relative phase with a periodic latent distribution whose small Fourier perturbations define the local two-quadrature experiment. At the uniform baseline,

`rho0=sum_n q_n |n><n|`.

The positive `k`-exchange tangent is

`A_k=sum_(n=0)^(L-k) sqrt(q_n q_(n+k)) |n+k><n|`.

This raises the signal by `k` quanta and lowers the clock by the same amount; every tangent remains in the fixed total-energy shell.

This is the autonomous relative-time counterpart of the Rev11 periodic random-time experiment.

## 2. One fixed record and the finite exchange shift

Define the finite shift

`V_L=sum_(n=0)^(L-1) |n+1><n|`.

Then

`A_k=rho0^(1/2) V_L^k rho0^(1/2)`.

For one fixed one-copy POVM, the Rev9/Rev10 posterior-state argument gives

`R_M(k)=int |Tr(V_L^k X_y)|^2 p(dy)`

with `X_y` posterior density operators.

The same expression has a Herglotz representation on the integer harmonics, so the retention values are positive definite. The crucial extra fact here is that `V_L` is finite nilpotent because the autonomous apparatus has a hard total-energy cap.

## 3. Exact numerical radius of the finite shift

For the `(L+1)`-dimensional unilateral shift,

`w(V_L)=cos[pi/(L+2)]`.

This is classical operator theory and equivalent to the standard finite-support optimal phase-estimation/sine-state result. It is **not** claimed as new.

For every density operator `X_y`,

`|Tr(V_L X_y)| <= w(V_L)`.

Therefore

> **Exact first-harmonic autonomous retention ceiling**
>
> `R_M(1) <= cos^2[pi/(L+2)]`.

The result holds for **every fixed one-copy POVM**, not only a covariant or canonical phase measurement.

Equivalently,

`L >= pi/arccos(sqrt(R_M(1))) - 2`.

Since the apparatus total excess energy is exactly

`E_tot^+=hbar omega0 L`,

we have the exact energy form

> `E_tot^+ >= hbar nu [ pi/arccos(sqrt(R_M(nu))) - 2 ]`
>
> for the fundamental relative frequency `nu=omega0`.

## 4. Exact higher-harmonic ceiling

For harmonic `k`, `V_L^k` decomposes into `k` independent residue chains. The longest chain has

`m_k+1` basis vectors, with

`m_k=floor(L/k)`

edges.

The numerical radius is therefore

`w(V_L^k)=cos[pi/(floor(L/k)+2)]`.

Hence

> **Exact hard-cap spectral law**
>
> `R_M(k) <= cos^2{ pi/[floor(L/k)+2] }`.

Writing `nu=k omega0`, this says that the maximum retained temporal Fisher fraction at a relative frequency `nu` depends only on how many full exchange quanta of size `hbar nu` fit inside the total apparatus energy cap.

For a hard cap `E_max=hbar omega0 L`,

`floor(L/k)=floor(E_max/(hbar nu))`,

so

> `R_M(nu) <= cos^2{ pi/[floor(E_max/(hbar nu))+2] }`.

This formulation also applies to mixtures supported across several total-energy shells provided no shell exceeds the same hard cap: the joint exchange shift is a direct sum, and the numerical radius is controlled by the longest allowed chain.

## 5. Exact saturation by autonomous sine-chain history states

For `k=1`, choose

`a_n=sqrt[2/(L+2)] sin[(n+1)pi/(L+2)]`,

`q_n=a_n^2`.

The globally stationary pure seed is

`|Psi_L>=sum_(n=0)^L a_n |L-n>_C |n>_S`.

This is an energy-entangled clock--signal history state entirely inside one total-energy eigenspace.

The canonical **relative phase** POVM on that shell gives posterior phase states with

`Tr(V_L X_theta)=exp(-i theta) cos[pi/(L+2)]`.

Therefore

`R(1)=cos^2[pi/(L+2)]`,

and the bound is exactly saturated.

No external clock is needed for the measurement: every POVM element can be chosen within the fixed-total-energy shell and hence commutes with global time translation.

For harmonic `k`, saturation is obtained by restricting to one longest residue chain modulo `k` and using the corresponding finite sine profile.

The sine-state construction is established phase-estimation prior art. Its role here is as the exact autonomous relational Fisher-retention extremizer.

## 6. Near-lossless asymptotic law

For `R=R_M(1)` near one,

`1-R >= sin^2[pi/(L+2)]`.

Thus

`E_tot^+ >= hbar nu [pi/arccos(sqrt R)-2]`

and, as `R->1`,

> `E_tot^+ >= pi hbar nu / sqrt(1-R) [1+o(1)]`.

The sine-chain family attains the same asymptotic coefficient `pi`, so the hard-cap theorem is **fully sharp**, including the leading constant.

This is stronger than the generic WP03 mean-energy result

`Ebar_C^+ + Ebar_S^+ >= sqrt(2) hbar nu / sqrt(1-R) [1+o(1)]`

for structured Herglotz retention, because a hard total-energy cap contains more information than a first moment.

## 7. Relation to established phase-estimation bounds

Finite sine states and the associated exact finite-support phase sharpness are standard results in phase estimation. Berry, Hall, Zwierz, and Wiseman, Phys. Rev. A 86, 053813 (2012), DOI `10.1103/PhysRevA.86.053813`, discuss optimal Heisenberg-style phase bounds for arbitrary measurements; finite-support canonical phase results yield the familiar `pi/(N+2)` scale. The same sine states also appear in optimal quantum clock/interferometric constructions.

Therefore the mathematical constant `pi` and the sine eigenvector are not novelty claims.

The candidate new statement is the identification

`fixed autonomous total-energy shell -> finite exchange shift -> exact arbitrary-POVM Fisher-retention ceiling`

for **globally time-translation-invariant relational temporal information**.

This should be positioned as an operational bridge between temporal Fisher-resource laws and relational/phase-estimation theory, not as a new number--phase uncertainty relation.

## 8. Mean-energy-only problem remains open

A hard cap is stronger than fixing only the mean total energy.

Known phase-estimation theory proves Heisenberg-style bounds under a mean generator constraint, including an asymptotically optimal Airy-function constant for suitable averaged phase-error metrics. It is not automatic that those results translate sharply to the present Fisher-retention functional

`R_M(k)=int |Tr(V^k X_y)|^2 p(dy)`

because the posterior energy distribution can vary with the measurement outcome and the functional averages **squared sharpness**.

The sharp mean-energy constant for autonomous relational Fisher retention is therefore still open and must not be inferred from the hard-cap result without proof.
