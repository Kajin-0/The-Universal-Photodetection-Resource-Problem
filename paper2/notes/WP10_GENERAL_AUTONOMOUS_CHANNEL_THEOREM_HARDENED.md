# WP10 — Hardened General Autonomous-Channel Fisher-Spectrum Theorem

**Status:** proof architecture upgraded to a theorem-grade whole-line local formulation using compactly supported source tangents. The remaining issue is novelty/positioning, not the central functional-analytic derivation.

## 1. Why this formulation is preferable

Earlier drafts worried about defining Fisher-information **rates** for infinite sinusoids and about increasing-window limits before the basic operator theorem was established.

That is unnecessary.

The mathematically clean primitive problem is local perturbation by

\[
u\in\mathcal D\equiv L^2(\mathbb R)\cap L^\infty(\mathbb R)
\]

with compact support (or any equivalent dense bounded-support class).

For sufficiently small `epsilon`,

\[
\lambda_\epsilon(t)=\Phi_0[1+\epsilon u(t)]
\]

is a valid Poisson intensity on the whole real line.

The exact arbitrary-waveform Fisher operator is proved on this dense class, extended by continuity to all `L^2`, and **only afterward** interpreted at a single frequency by narrowband limits.

This removes the need to place the physical detector on a finite torus or to assume an infinite sinusoid is itself an admissible perturbation.

## 2. Source DQM on configuration space

Let `P_{epsilon,u}` be the law of a Poisson point process `N` on `R` with intensity

\[
\Phi_0[1+\epsilon u(t)].
\]

For bounded compactly supported `u`, the likelihood relative to the homogeneous baseline `P_0` is

\[
\log\frac{dP_{\epsilon,u}}{dP_0}(N)
=
\int \log[1+\epsilon u(t)]N(dt)
-\Phi_0\epsilon\int u(t)dt.
\]

Standard Poisson likelihood expansion gives differentiability in quadratic mean at `epsilon=0` with score

\[
\boxed{
S_u(N)=\int u(t)[N(dt)-\Phi_0dt].
}
\]

Poisson isometry gives

\[
\boxed{
E_0[S_uS_v]
=\Phi_0\langle u,v\rangle_{L^2}.
}
\]

Thus the closed source score space generated by these tangents is isometric, after division by `sqrt(Phi0)`, to scalar real `L^2(R)`.

The compactly supported bounded class is dense in `L^2`, so any bounded quadratic form obtained below extends uniquely to all `L^2`.

## 3. Arbitrary parameter-independent detector channel

Let

\[
K(dy|n)
\]

be **any** parameter-independent Markov kernel from the incident point configuration `N` to an accessible detector record `Y`.

No assumptions are made here about:

- independent events;
- low flux;
- one output per photon;
- renewal structure;
- finite detector state;
- dead-time model;
- analog versus digital output;
- existence of a single-event IRF.

Let

\[
Q_{\epsilon,u}=P_{\epsilon,u}K
\]

be the induced output experiment.

A standard DQM theorem for parameter-independent Markov images gives the output score

\[
\boxed{
S_u^{\rm out}(Y)
=E_0[S_u(N)|Y].
}
\]

This identity and Fisher monotonicity are **prior art**, not novelty claims.

## 4. Exact waveform Fisher-retention operator

Define

\[
\mathcal F_K[u,v]
=E_0[S_u^{\rm out}S_v^{\rm out}].
\]

Conditional expectation is an `L^2` contraction, hence

\[
|\mathcal F_K[u,v]|
\le
\Phi_0\|u\|_2\|v\|_2.
\]

Therefore, by Riesz representation, there is a unique bounded self-adjoint positive operator

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_K:L^2(\mathbb R)\to L^2(\mathbb R)
}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_K[u,v]
=\Phi_0\langle u,\mathcal A_Kv\rangle.
}
\]

For every `u`,

\[
0\le\mathcal F_K[u,u]
\le\Phi_0\|u\|_2^2,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{0\preceq\mathcal A_K\preceq I.}
\]

This is the complete local Fisher-retention operator of the detector channel on the Poisson temporal source tangent.

## 5. Autonomy implies exact commutation with translations

Let `Theta_a` shift an input or output trajectory by `a`, and let

\[
(U_au)(t)=u(t-a)
\]

be the corresponding unitary representation on `L^2(R)`.

Assume the detector channel is autonomous / time-translation covariant:

\[
\boxed{
K(\Theta_a B\mid\Theta_a n)=K(B\mid n)
}
\]

for all shifts and relevant measurable records/configurations, up to the usual null-set formulation.

The homogeneous Poisson baseline is stationary, and the joint baseline law of `(N,Y)` is therefore stationary.

The source scores transform covariantly:

\[
S_{U_au}(N)
=S_u(\Theta_{-a}N)
\]

under a consistent shift convention. Covariance of conditional expectation under the stationary joint law gives

\[
\mathcal F_K[U_au,U_av]
=\mathcal F_K[u,v].
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_KU_a=U_a\mathcal A_K
\qquad\forall a\in\mathbb R.
}
\]

This is the only detector-specific structural assumption needed to force frequency diagonalization.

## 6. Fourier diagonalization

A bounded operator on scalar `L^2(R)` commuting with all translations is a Fourier multiplier. Thus, after complexifying the real Hilbert space, there exists

\[
G_K\in L^\infty(\mathbb R)
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathcal A_Ku}(\omega)
=G_K(\omega)\widehat u(\omega)
\quad\text{a.e.}
}
\]

Because `A_K` is self-adjoint and positive contractive,

\[
\boxed{
0\le G_K(\omega)\le1
\quad\text{a.e.}
}
\]

Because the original operator maps real waveforms to real waveforms,

\[
G_K(-\omega)=\overline{G_K(\omega)}.
\]

Self-adjointness makes `G_K` real a.e., hence

\[
\boxed{G_K(-\omega)=G_K(\omega)\quad\text{a.e.}}
\]

The dependence on baseline flux and detector operating point is implicit; in applications write `G_{Phi0,K}(omega)`.

## 7. Complete arbitrary-waveform theorem

With Fourier convention

\[
U(\omega)=\int u(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt,
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\boxed{
F_{\rm out}[u,v]
=\frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi}
\int_{\mathbb R}
G_{\Phi_0,K}(\omega)
U^*(\omega)V(\omega)d\omega.
}
\]

For any finite tangent family `{u_a}`,

\[
\boxed{
[F_{\rm out}]_{ab}
=\frac{\Phi_0}{2\pi}
\int G_{\Phi_0,K}(\omega)
U_a^*(\omega)U_b(\omega)d\omega.
}
\]

### Hardened theorem statement

> **General autonomous photodetection Fisher-spectrum theorem.** Let a homogeneous Poisson optical source of baseline flux `Phi0` be perturbed locally by bounded compactly supported intensity waveforms. Let a parameter-independent stochastic detector channel map the complete incident configuration to an accessible record, and assume the baseline channel is covariant under global time translations. Then the complete local output Fisher metric extends uniquely to a positive contraction on the source `L^2` waveform tangent and is represented by an even measurable scalar multiplier `G_{Phi0,K}(omega)` satisfying `0<=G<=1` a.e. The displayed spectral integral gives the output Fisher matrix for every finite family of admitted local waveforms.

The theorem permits arbitrary detector memory and high-flux nonlinear dynamics.

## 8. Long sinusoidal tests are a consequence, not an assumption

A pure sinusoid on the whole line is not in `L^2`. Its information **rate** should therefore be obtained as a limit of long narrowband packets.

Let `w` be a fixed square-integrable window and define

\[
u_T(t)
=T^{-1/2}w(t/T)e^{i\omega_0t}.
\]

Then `||u_T||_2=||w||_2` and

\[
|U_T(\omega)|^2
\]

concentrates around `omega_0` as `T->infinity`.

At every Lebesgue point of `G`,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{F_{\rm out}[u_T,u_T]}
{F_{\rm in}[u_T,u_T]}
\longrightarrow G(\omega_0).
}
\]

For real cosine windows the spectrum concentrates at `+-omega_0`; evenness of `G` gives the same limit.

Thus `G(omega)` has an operational single-frequency meaning without ever putting a non-`L^2` sinusoid into the primitive theorem.

This also clarifies why the general theorem guarantees `G` only a.e.; continuity requires extra detector-specific mixing/regularity.

## 9. Universal local detector ordering

For detector channels `A` and `B` at the same source operating point,

\[
F_A[u,u]\ge F_B[u,u]
\quad\forall u
\]

iff

\[
\mathcal A_A-\mathcal A_B\succeq0.
\]

Since both are multipliers,

\[
\boxed{
A\succeq_{\rm local\ FI}B
\iff
G_A(\omega)\ge G_B(\omega)
\quad\text{a.e.}
}
\]

for **every admitted local temporal waveform task**.

This is not generic Blackwell dominance.

## 10. Frequency-by-frequency data processing

Suppose the fine detector record `Y` is passed through an additional parameter-independent autonomous channel to a coarse record `Z`.

Conditional expectation nesting gives

\[
E[S_u|Z]=E[E[S_u|Y]|Z].
\]

Therefore

\[
\mathcal A_Y\succeq\mathcal A_Z.
\]

Autonomy diagonalizes both, yielding

\[
\boxed{
G_Y(\omega)\ge G_Z(\omega)
\quad\text{a.e.}
}
\]

This is the general-channel extension of Paper 1's mark-refinement inequality.

## 11. Exact recovery of Paper 1

In Paper 1's low-overlap independent-event model, each incident photon independently produces either no record or one retained marked event with capture measure `kappa(dm)` and delay law `mu_m(d tau)`.

The output is a marked Poisson process. For source perturbation `u`, its marked output intensity is

\[
\Lambda_\epsilon(m,t)
=\Phi_0\kappa(dm)
\left[1+\epsilon\int u(t-\tau)\mu_m(d\tau)\right].
\]

The local Fisher form of a marked Poisson process is

\[
F_{\rm out}[u,v]
=\Phi_0\int\kappa(dm)
\int (u*\mu_m)(t)(v*\mu_m)(t)dt.
\]

Parseval gives

\[
\boxed{
G_{\Phi_0,K}(\omega)
=\int |H_m(\omega)|^2\kappa(dm),
}
\]

exactly reproducing Paper 1. In this independent-event special case the multiplier is flux independent.

Thus Paper 1 is a genuine soluble subtheory of the general channel theorem.

## 12. Nonautonomous and multimode extensions

### Nonautonomous detector

Without translation covariance, `A_K` remains the correct positive contraction but is not generally diagonal in frequency. A two-frequency kernel should be written only when additional kernel regularity exists.

### Multiple source channels

For `d` independently controllable temporal source components, the tangent space is `L^2(R;C^d)`. Translation covariance gives a matrix-valued multiplier

\[
\boxed{
0\preceq\mathbf G(\omega)\preceq I_d.
}
\]

This is the rigorous operator-valued generalization suggested at the start of Paper 2.

## 13. What is standard and what may be new

Standard ingredients:

- Poisson DQM and compensated-integral scores;
- Markov-image score = conditional expectation;
- Fisher data processing;
- Riesz representation of bounded quadratic forms;
- Fourier representation of translation-invariant `L^2` operators.

Potentially new combined photodetection statement:

> arbitrary autonomous detector memory and high-flux nonlinear dynamics still admit a complete scalar local temporal Fisher-transfer spectrum because the **source tangent**, not the detector dynamics, has multiplicity one under temporal translations.

The theorem's significance depends on this combination plus consequences such as WP06/WP07, not on any individual mathematical ingredient.

## 14. Remaining rigor gates

The central proof no longer needs an increasing-window construction. Remaining formal tasks are narrower:

1. Cite a theorem-grade DQM-under-Markov-kernel source.
2. State the trajectory spaces and shift covariance measurably enough for publication.
3. Cite the translation-invariant multiplier theorem.
4. Specify a dense admissible perturbation class and the extension to `L^2` cleanly.
5. For physical sinusoidal language, state the narrowband/Lebesgue-point corollary rather than treating pure sinusoids as primitive admissible waveforms.
6. If continuity of `G` is desired, prove it separately for specified mixing detector classes; do not include continuity in the universal theorem.

These are formalization tasks, not conceptual gaps.
