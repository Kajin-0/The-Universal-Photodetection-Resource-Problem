`boxed:`

`boxed: G_DC = G_cyc = (r/lambda) I_D`.

Therefore the WP25 finite-mean singularity theorem immediately upgrades to the stationary fixed-window experiment:

`boxed:`

This holds throughout the finite-mean iid-recovery Type-II class, including atomic, singular, infinite-variance, and heavy-tailed recovery laws.

# Part I — why the generic renewal theorem did not already settle this

## 2. Zhao–Nagaraja 2011 requires a forward-recurrence regularity condition

Zhao & Nagaraja, **“Fisher information in window censored renewal process data and its applications,”** Ann. Inst. Stat. Math. 63, 791–825 (2011), DOI `10.1007/s10463-009-0252-2`, study exactly a stationary/random-origin renewal process observed in a finite window.

Their Theorem 3 states, under their regularity conditions,

`lim_{L->infinity} I_window(L)/L = I_D/E[D]`.

Their listed assumptions include:

- finite mean inter-renewal time;
- regular derivatives of the forward-recurrence density;
- **finite Fisher information in the forward recurrence time**.

Thus their theorem cannot simply be cited as proving the result for every finite-mean heavy-tailed renewal law.

This matters because if `E[D^2]=infinity`, the stationary forward recurrence can have infinite mean, and generic renewal boundary arguments may be delicate.

The Type-II detector has extra stationary latent structure that removes this obstruction.

# Part II — ordinary renewal process started at a renewal

## 3. DQM interval experiment from WP25

WP25 proves that for every finite-mean recovery law, the observed Palm interval family `P_epsilon^D` is DQM in the fractional rate parameter. Let

`a(D)`

be the one-interval score and

`I_D=E[a(D)^2]<infinity`.

Moreover

`I_D<=lambda/r`.

No density for `D` is required.

## 4. Progressive right-censoring of one interval

For `t>=0`, define the censoring statistic

`C_t(D)`

that reveals `D` exactly when `D<=t` and otherwise reports only `D>t`.

Because `C_t` is a parameter-independent statistic of a DQM experiment, its score is

`a_t(C_t)=E[a(D)|C_t(D)]`

and its Fisher information is

`J(t)=E[a_t(C_t)^2]`.

The sigma fields generated by `C_t(D)` increase with `t` and converge to `sigma(D)`. Hence by `L2` martingale convergence,

`boxed: J(t) increases to I_D as t->infinity`.

Also

`0<=J(t)<=I_D`.

## 5. Ordinary right-censored renewal experiment

Start a renewal process at an observed renewal at time zero and observe it until deterministic horizon `t`.

Generate iid intervals `D_1,D_2,...`. Before draw `n`, let

`R_n=t-S_{n-1}`,

where

`S_{n-1}=D_1+...+D_{n-1}`.

If `R_n>0`, the next observation is exactly the censored statistic

`C_{R_n}(D_n)`.

If the draw is censored, the experiment stops.

At each stage the conditional score has zero conditional mean and conditional Fisher information `J(R_n)`. Therefore the likelihood-score increments are orthogonal and

`boxed:`

`I_ord(t)=E[ sum_{n>=1} 1{S_{n-1}<=t} J(t-S_{n-1}) ]`.

Let `N_t=max{n:S_n<=t}` be the number of complete renewals by time `t`. Then there are `N_t+1` possible stages.

### Upper bound

Since `J<=I_D`,

`I_ord(t)<=I_D E[N_t+1]`.

### Lower bound

Fix `R>0`. Every stage beginning by time `t-R` has residual horizon at least `R`, so

`I_ord(t)>=J(R) E[N_{t-R}+1]`

for `t>=R`.

By the elementary renewal theorem, using only `E[D]<infinity`,

`E[N_t]/t -> 1/E[D] = r`.

Hence

`limsup I_ord(t)/t <= r I_D`,

while for every fixed `R`,

`liminf I_ord(t)/t >= r J(R)`.

Taking `R->infinity` gives

`boxed:`

`lim_{t->infinity} I_ord(t)/t = r I_D`.

This proof is measure-theoretic and does not require a density, second moment, forward-recurrence moment, or finite FRT Fisher information.

# Part III — the stationary left boundary is a finite latent Poisson experiment

## 6. Stationary Type-II state at an arbitrary time

Represent the detector by the stationary marked Poisson input

`Pi=sum_i delta_(S_i,T_i)`

on `R x [0,infinity)` with intensity

`lambda ds F(dT)`.

At time zero, the only pre-zero events capable of affecting future detector state are those whose recovery intervals are still active:

`A_0={(s,T): s<=0<s+T}`.

The intensity measure of this region is

`lambda int F(dT) int_{-T}^0 ds = lambda E[T] = lambda m`.

Therefore the stationary active cloud is a finite Poisson random measure. Its count

`K_0~Poisson(lambda m)`.

For the fractional rate parameter, its score is

`S_A=K_0-lambda m`

with Fisher information

`boxed: E[S_A^2]=lambda m`.

This is the exact stationary `M/G/infinity` occupancy insensitivity result in point-process form. Only finite `m` is required.

The active residual/age/mark configuration conditional on `K_0` is parameter-independent because the parameter scales only the Poisson intensity.

## 7. Censored first registered event has sublinear information

Let `Y` be the stationary forward recurrence time to the next registered cluster start and define

`tau_L=min(Y,L)`.

The censored first-event observation `C_L(Y)` is determined by:

1. the finite active cloud `A_0`;
2. future marked-Poisson arrivals up to stopping time `tau_L`.

Conditional on `A_0`, `tau_L` is a bounded stopping time of the future marked Poisson process.

The future fractional-rate score is

`M_{tau_L}=N_{tau_L}-lambda tau_L`.

Optional isometry gives

`E[M_{tau_L}^2|A_0]=lambda E[tau_L|A_0]`.

Also

`E[M_{tau_L}|A_0]=0`,

so the initial-cloud score is orthogonal to the stopped future score. The latent experiment generating `C_L(Y)` therefore has FI

`lambda m + lambda E[min(Y,L)]`.

By data processing,

`boxed:`

`I(C_L(Y)) <= lambda m + lambda E[min(Y,L)]`.

Now `Y<infinity` almost surely because the registered renewal process has positive finite intensity `r`. Hence

`min(Y,L)/L ->0` almost surely

and is bounded by one. Dominated convergence gives

`E[min(Y,L)]/L ->0`.

Therefore

`boxed: I(C_L(Y))/L ->0`.

This remains true even if

`E[Y]=infinity`,

which is exactly the heavy-tail case that generic FRT-moment arguments do not cover.

# Part IV — stationary window decomposition

## 8. Exact chain-rule decomposition

Observe the stationary registered renewal process in `[0,L]`.

The first stage is `C_L(Y)`.

- If `Y>=L`, there is no registered event in the window and the experiment ends.
- If `Y=y<L`, the post-`y` process is a fresh ordinary renewal process started at a registered event, independent of `Y`, observed for remaining duration `L-y`.

DQM chain rule therefore gives the exact Fisher-information decomposition

`boxed:`

`I_stat(L)=I(C_L(Y)) + E[ 1{Y<L} I_ord(L-Y) ]`.

The two score components are orthogonal because the conditional post-first-event score has zero mean given the first-stage observation.

---

## 9. Random remaining horizon does not alter the bulk rate

From Part II,

`I_ord(t)/t -> c`,

where

`c=r I_D`.

The upper bound

`I_ord(t)<=I_D(E[N_t]+1)`

and the elementary renewal theorem imply there exists a finite constant `C` such that

`I_ord(t)<=C(1+t)`

for all `t>=0`.

Fix `delta in (0,1)` and split according to `Y<=delta L` and `Y>delta L`.

### Main region `Y<=delta L`

There

`L-Y >= (1-delta)L`.

Since scalar convergence `I_ord(t)/t ->c` is uniform over the tail `t>=(1-delta)L`,

`I_ord(L-Y)/L = [I_ord(L-Y)/(L-Y)] [1-Y/L]`

converges uniformly in the first factor to `c`.

Also

`(1-Y/L)1{Y<=delta L}->1`

almost surely and is bounded by one. Hence

`E[1{Y<=delta L} I_ord(L-Y)/L] -> c`.

### Tail region `Y>delta L`

Using the linear bound,

`0<=E[1{delta L<Y<L} I_ord(L-Y)/L]`

`<=C' P(Y>delta L)+C'/L ->0`,

because `Y` is proper.

Therefore

`boxed:`

`E[1{Y<L} I_ord(L-Y)]/L -> r I_D`.

Combining with the sublinear first-stage information from Part III gives

`boxed:`

`lim_{L->infinity} I_stat(L)/L = r I_D`.
