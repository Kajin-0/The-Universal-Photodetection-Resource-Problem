\subsection{Selected carrier--sideband pair with arbitrary spectators}

Let $|c\rangle$ denote a selected carrier frequency bin and $|s\rangle$ a sideband bin separated by angular frequency $\Omega$. All other populated modes are collected into an orthogonal spectator subspace $\mathcal H_\perp$. For $p>0$, consider a baseline
\begin{equation}
\rho_p=a_p|c\rangle\langle c|+p|s\rangle\langle s|+\sigma_p,
\label{eq:rho_p_general}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_p\succeq0$ is supported on $\mathcal H_\perp$,
\begin{equation}
a_p+p+\Tr\sigma_p=1,
\end{equation}
and, as $p\to0^+$,
\begin{equation}
a_p\to q>0,
\qquad
\sigma_p\to\sigma_0
\quad\text{in trace norm}.
\label{eq:seed_path_limit}
\end{equation}
The seed can therefore be compensated anywhere in the spectator population; it need not be taken entirely from the selected carrier. We assume only $a_p>p$ in the neighborhood of interest.

Apply a calibrated lossless converter acting solely on the selected pair,
\begin{equation}
U(x,y)=\exp\!\left\{\kappa[(x-iy)|s\rangle\langle c|-(x+iy)|c\rangle\langle s|]\right\},
\label{eq:converter_general}
\end{equation}
where $x,y$ are dimensionless peak control quadratures, $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, and $\kappa$ converts control amplitude to a small mixing angle. The spectator block is inert.

The selected sideband population is exactly
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
P_s(p;r)=p+(a_p-p)\sin^2(\kappa r).}
\label{eq:sideband_population_general}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
P_s=p+(a_p-p)\kappa^2(x^2+y^2)+O(r^4).
\end{equation}

The first-order affine $c$--$s$ block is
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}
a_p & \kappa(a_p-p)(x+iy)\\
\kappa(a_p-p)(x-iy) & p
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
up to an immaterial sine-quadrature sign convention. Its determinant is
\begin{equation}
a_pp-\kappa^2(a_p-p)^2(x^2+y^2).
\end{equation}
Because the spectator sector is positive and uncoupled, the exact affine physical radius is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{\rm lin}^2=
\frac{a_pp}{\kappa^2(a_p-p)^2}.}
\label{eq:Rlin_general}
\end{equation}

For every $p>0$, the positive-frequency tangent has range in the pre-existing sideband sector. The finite-radius spectral-survival theorem of Ref.~\cite{CompanionTwoRegimes2026} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{R_{\rm lin}^2}{4}\Tr F\le p.}
\label{eq:survival_bound_general}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\Tr F\le
4\kappa^2\frac{(a_p-p)^2}{a_p}.
\label{eq:survival_ceiling_general}
\end{equation}
The resource on the right of Eq.~\eqref{eq:survival_bound_general} is simply the directly measurable baseline population of the selected sideband.

\subsection{Spectator-independent support-to-synthesis crossover}

At $p=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed_path_limit} gives selected carrier population $q$ and an empty sideband. Equation~\eqref{eq:sideband_population_general} becomes
\begin{equation}
P_s(0;x,y)=q\kappa^2(x^2+y^2)+O(r^4),
\end{equation}
so with $\Delta=\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Delta P_s(0)=4\kappa^2q.}
\label{eq:sideband_curvature_general}
\end{equation}
The corresponding one-sided rank-boundary inequality is
\begin{equation}
\Tr F\le\Delta P_s(0).
\label{eq:boundary_bound_general}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Spectator-independent seed regularization]
For the family \eqref{eq:rho_p_general}--\eqref{eq:converter_general},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\lim_{p\to0^+}\frac{4p}{R_{\rm lin}^2}
=4\kappa^2q
=\Delta P_s(0).}
\label{eq:main_crossover_general}
\end{equation}
The limit is independent of the spectator populations and of the detailed seed-compensation path, subject to Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed_path_limit} and the assumption that the local converter leaves the spectator sector inert.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rlin_general} gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{4p}{R_{\rm lin}^2}
=4\kappa^2\frac{(a_p-p)^2}{a_p}.
\end{equation}
Since $a_p\to q>0$, the right-hand side tends to $4\kappa^2q$. Equation~\eqref{eq:sideband_curvature_general} gives the same quantity from the exact zero-seed family.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:main_crossover_general} is the practical content of the survival/synthesis distinction. As the selected sideband seed is removed, the pre-existing upper-endpoint population and the affine physical radius vanish together. Their radius-normalized ratio converges to the directly measurable second-order generation curvature of the now-empty sideband. Correspondingly, the finite-radius information ceiling \eqref{eq:survival_ceiling_general} tends continuously to the boundary ceiling \eqref{eq:boundary_bound_general}, even though the order of the resource changes.

The theorem does not require the selected carrier to contain the full baseline population. Populated polarization, spatial, spectral, or other spectator modes are permitted. What is required is a calibrated local two-mode converter whose first-order action is confined to the selected carrier/sideband pair. Loss, parameter-dependent decoherence, seed-dependent coupling to spectators, or other channel changes constitute Level-I model departures and need not obey the exact equalities above.

At the boundary, the active $c$--$s$ block has weight $q$ and local form
\begin{equation}
q\,|\psi(x,y)\rangle\langle\psi(x,y)|,
\qquad
|\psi(x,y)\rangle
=|c\rangle+\kappa(x-iy)|s\rangle+O(r^2),
\end{equation}
plus the parameter-independent spectator state $\sigma_0$. A fixed four-outcome equatorial POVM on the active two-mode subspace, completed by the spectator projector, gives
\begin{equation}
F_{xx}=F_{yy}=2q\kappa^2,
\qquad F_{xy}=0,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Tr F=4q\kappa^2=\Delta P_s(0).}
\label{eq:boundary_saturation_general}
\end{equation}
Thus the generalized boundary law remains attainable despite arbitrary inert spectator population.

For visualization and the simplest laboratory normalization, one may take
\begin{equation}
a_p=1-p,
\qquad \sigma_p=0,
\end{equation}
which reduces Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Rlin_general} and \eqref{eq:main_crossover_general} to
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm lin}^2=
\frac{p(1-p)}{\kappa^2(1-2p)^2},
\qquad
\lim_{p\to0^+}\frac{4p}{R_{\rm lin}^2}=4\kappa^2.
\end{equation}
This special case is useful for figures but is not required by the proposition.

\subsection{Ordinary phase modulation as a bilateral boundary example}

The same boundary structure appears in textbook optical phase modulation. Starting from a single carrier and applying
\begin{equation}
\exp\{i[x\cos(\Omega t)+y\sin(\Omega t)]\},
\end{equation}
the first upper and lower sideband probabilities obey
\begin{equation}
P_+(x,y)=P_-(x,y)=\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}+O[(x^2+y^2)^2].
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\Delta P_+(0)=\Delta P_-(0)=1.
\end{equation}
The bilateral boundary inequality of Ref.~\cite{CompanionTwoRegimes2026} becomes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Tr F\le
\left[\sqrt{\Delta P_+(0)}+\sqrt{\Delta P_-(0)}\right]^2=4.}
\label{eq:bilateral_bound_r2}
\end{equation}
The local pure-state QFI has $H_{xx}=H_{yy}=2$ and $H_{xy}=0$, and a fixed three-mode interferometric measurement can attain the same classical Fisher matrix. Thus ideal weak phase modulation saturates Eq.~\eqref{eq:bilateral_bound_r2}.

The resource and Fisher measurements need not be the same measurement. Direct spectral photon counting or power analysis measures the sideband curvature. Recovering both modulation quadratures at first order requires a phase-sensitive measurement, such as calibrated carrier/sideband mixing, frequency-bin interferometry, homodyne detection, or an equivalent POVM. This separation is important experimentally.
