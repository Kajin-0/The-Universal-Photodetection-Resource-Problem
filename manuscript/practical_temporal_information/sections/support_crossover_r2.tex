\subsection{Selected carrier--sideband pair with arbitrary stationary spectators}

Let $H$ denote the free optical Hamiltonian. Let $|c\rangle$ and $|s\rangle$ be selected energy/frequency modes satisfying $E_s-E_c=\hbar\Omega$, and collect all other populated modes into an orthogonal spectator subspace $\mathcal H_\perp$. For $p>0$, consider a stationary baseline
\begin{equation}
\rho_p=a_p|c\rangle\langle c|+p|s\rangle\langle s|+\sigma_p,
\label{eq:rho_p_general}
\end{equation}
with $[\rho_p,H]=0$. Here $\sigma_p\succeq0$ is supported on $\mathcal H_\perp$ and is itself stationary with respect to the free Hamiltonian, allowing coherence within degenerate energy subspaces but not arbitrary coherence between distinct free-energy eigenspaces. The normalization is
\begin{equation}
a_p+p+\Tr\sigma_p=1,
\end{equation}
and, as $p\to0^+$,
\begin{equation}
a_p\to q>0,
\qquad
\sigma_p\to\sigma_0
\quad\text{in trace norm}.
\label{eq:seed_path_limit}
\end{equation}
The seed can therefore be compensated anywhere in the stationary spectator population; it need not be taken entirely from the selected carrier. We assume only $a_p>p$ in the neighborhood of interest.

The parameter $p$ is an \emph{incoherent population seed}: the baseline has no fixed carrier--sideband coherence. Experimentally this can be realized by an independently populated sideband, relative-phase randomization, or another preparation whose ensemble state is diagonal in the selected frequency basis. A fixed coherent sideband amplitude changes the baseline off-diagonal structure and is outside the exact model considered here.

Apply a calibrated lossless converter acting solely on the selected pair,
\begin{equation}
U(x,y)=\exp\!\left\{\kappa[(x-iy)|s\rangle\langle c|-(x+iy)|c\rangle\langle s|]\right\},
\label{eq:converter_general}
\end{equation}
where $x,y$ are dimensionless peak control quadratures, $r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$, and $\kappa$ converts control amplitude to a small mixing angle. The spectator block is inert. Because the selected modes are separated by $\hbar\Omega$, the positive-frequency first-order tangent is confined to the definite $+\Omega$ spectral mode required by the one-sided survival theorem.

The selected sideband population is exactly
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
P_s(p;r)=p+(a_p-p)\sin^2(\kappa r).}
\label{eq:sideband_population_general}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
P_s=p+(a_p-p)\kappa^2(x^2+y^2)+O(r^4).
\end{equation}

The first-order affine $c$--$s$ block is
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}
a_p & \kappa(a_p-p)(x+iy)\\
\kappa(a_p-p)(x-iy) & p
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
up to an immaterial sine-quadrature sign convention. Its determinant is
\begin{equation}
a_p\,p-\kappa^2(a_p-p)^2(x^2+y^2).
\end{equation}
Because the spectator sector is positive and uncoupled, the exact affine physical radius is therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
R_{\rm lin}^2=
\frac{a_p\,p}{\kappa^2(a_p-p)^2}.}
\label{eq:Rlin_general}
\end{equation}

Operationally, $R_{\rm lin}$ should not be inferred from the zero-seed curvature that it is later compared with. It can be reconstructed independently from the baseline state and the first derivatives $D_x=\partial_x\rho|_0$ and $D_y=\partial_y\rho|_0$: local state/tangent tomography determines the largest disk for which $\rho_p+xD_x+yD_y\succeq0$. In the selected two-mode model this independent reconstruction reduces to Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rlin_general} once $a_p$, $p$, and $\kappa$ have been separately calibrated from baseline populations and the small-signal converter response.

For every $p>0$, the positive-frequency tangent has range in the pre-existing sideband sector. The finite-radius spectral-survival theorem of Ref.~\cite{CompanionTwoRegimes2026} gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{R_{\rm lin}^2}{4}\Tr F\le p.}
\label{eq:survival_bound_general}
\label{eq:survival_bound}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\Tr F\le
4\kappa^2\frac{(a_p-p)^2}{a_p}.
\label{eq:survival_ceiling_general}
\end{equation}
The resource on the right of Eq.~\eqref{eq:survival_bound_general} is simply the independently measurable baseline population of the selected upper sideband.

\subsection{Spectator-independent support-to-synthesis crossover}

At $p=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed_path_limit} gives selected carrier population $q$ and an empty sideband. Equation~\eqref{eq:sideband_population_general} becomes
\begin{equation}
P_s(0;x,y)=q\kappa^2(x^2+y^2)+O(r^4),
\end{equation}
so with $\Delta=\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Delta P_s(0)=4\kappa^2q.}
\label{eq:sideband_curvature_general}
\end{equation}
The corresponding one-sided rank-boundary inequality is
\begin{equation}
\Tr F\le\Delta P_s(0).
\label{eq:boundary_bound_general}
\label{eq:boundary_bound}
\end{equation}

Finite Fisher information associated with an outcome or eigenvalue that vanishes quadratically at a rank-changing point is already known in quantum sensing and boundary-QFI theory \cite{GefenRotemRetzker2019,Safranek2017}. Our added statement is not that boundary mechanism itself. It is the finite-seed continuation from the interior: the pre-existing sideband population and its exact affine physical radius can be followed continuously to zero, and their radius-normalized survival resource converges to the boundary population curvature.

\begin{proposition}[Spectator-independent seed regularization]
For the stationary family \eqref{eq:rho_p_general}--\eqref{eq:converter_general},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\lim_{p\to0^+}\frac{4p}{R_{\rm lin}^2}
=4\kappa^2q
=\Delta P_s(0).}
\label{eq:main_crossover_general}
\label{eq:main_crossover}
\end{equation}
The limit is independent of the stationary spectator populations and of the detailed seed-compensation path, subject to Eq.~\eqref{eq:seed_path_limit} and the assumption that the local converter leaves the spectator sector inert.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substitution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:Rlin_general} gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{4p}{R_{\rm lin}^2}
=4\kappa^2\frac{(a_p-p)^2}{a_p}.
\end{equation}
Since $a_p\to q>0$, the right-hand side tends to $4\kappa^2q$. Equation~\eqref{eq:sideband_curvature_general} gives the same quantity from the exact zero-seed family.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:main_crossover_general} is the practical content of the survival/synthesis distinction. As the selected sideband seed is removed, the pre-existing upper-endpoint population and the affine physical radius vanish together. Their radius-normalized ratio converges to the directly measurable second-order generation curvature of the now-empty sideband. Correspondingly, the finite-radius information ceiling \eqref{eq:survival_ceiling_general} tends continuously to the boundary ceiling \eqref{eq:boundary_bound_general}, even though the order of the resource changes.

A noncircular experimental comparison uses three independently obtained data products. First, baseline plus local tangent tomography determines $R_{\rm lin}$ without using second-order boundary data. Second, a separate zero-seed sweep fits $P_s(x,y)$ to obtain $\Delta P_s(0)$. Third, an independent phase-sensitive record supplies the Fisher matrix. Failure of the radius--curvature identity itself first falsifies the selected stationary two-mode model; only a Fisher-information excess under independently verified theorem hypotheses constitutes a Level-II resource-law challenge.

The theorem does not require the selected carrier to contain the full baseline population. Populated polarization, spatial, spectral, or other stationary spectator modes are permitted. What is required is a stationary baseline, a definite selected spectral gap, and a calibrated local two-mode converter whose first-order action is confined to that carrier/sideband pair. Loss, parameter-dependent decoherence, seed-dependent coupling to spectators, inter-energy spectator coherence, or other channel changes constitute Level-I model departures and need not obey the exact equalities above.

At the boundary, the active $c$--$s$ block has weight $q$. Write its normalized local state as
\begin{equation}
|\psi(x,y)\rangle
=|c\rangle+\kappa(x-iy)|s\rangle+O(r^2).
\end{equation}
The full boundary state is the active block $q|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|$ plus the parameter-independent spectator state $\sigma_0$. A fixed four-outcome equatorial POVM on the active two-mode subspace, completed by the spectator projector, gives
\begin{equation}
F_{xx}=F_{yy}=2q\kappa^2,
\qquad F_{xy}=0,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\Tr F=4q\kappa^2=\Delta P_s(0).}
\label{eq:boundary_saturation_general}
\end{equation}
Thus the generalized boundary law remains attainable despite arbitrary inert stationary spectator population.

For visualization and the simplest laboratory normalization, one may take
\begin{equation}
a_p=1-p,
\qquad \sigma_p=0,
\end{equation}
which reduces Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Rlin_general} and \eqref{eq:main_crossover_general} to
\begin{equation}
R_{\rm lin}^2=
\frac{p(1-p)}{\kappa^2(1-2p)^2},
\qquad
\lim_{p\to0^+}\frac{4p}{R_{\rm lin}^2}=4\kappa^2.
\end{equation}
This special case is useful for figures but is not required by the proposition.

\subsection{Ordinary phase modulation as a bilateral boundary example}

The same boundary structure appears in textbook optical phase modulation. Starting from a single carrier and applying
\begin{equation}
\exp\{i[x\cos(\Omega t)+y\sin(\Omega t)]\},
\end{equation}
the first upper and lower sideband probabilities obey
\begin{equation}
P_+(x,y)=P_-(x,y)=\frac{x^2+y^2}{4}+O[(x^2+y^2)^2].
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\Delta P_+(0)=\Delta P_-(0)=1.
\end{equation}
The bilateral boundary inequality of Ref.~\cite{CompanionTwoRegimes2026} becomes
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Tr F\le
\left[\sqrt{\Delta P_+(0)}+\sqrt{\Delta P_-(0)}\right]^2=4.}
\label{eq:bilateral_bound_r2}
\end{equation}
The local pure-state QFI has $H_{xx}=H_{yy}=2$ and $H_{xy}=0$, and a fixed three-mode interferometric measurement can attain the same classical Fisher matrix. Thus ideal weak phase modulation saturates Eq.~\eqref{eq:bilateral_bound_r2}.

The resource and Fisher measurements need not be the same measurement. Direct spectral photon counting or power analysis measures the sideband curvature. Recovering both modulation quadratures at first order requires a phase-sensitive measurement, such as calibrated carrier/sideband mixing, frequency-bin interferometry, homodyne detection, or an equivalent POVM. This separation is important experimentally.
