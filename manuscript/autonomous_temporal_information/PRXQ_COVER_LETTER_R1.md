Dear Editors,

Please consider the manuscript **“Two spectral-resource regimes for autonomous temporal information”** for publication as a Research Article in *PRX Quantum*.

The manuscript asks a resource question that arises when time is treated internally rather than supplied by an ideal external reference: what physical resource constrains information about relative time when a finite clock and signal can exchange energy while their joint state remains globally stationary? This setting makes global time-translation asymmetry insufficient as a universal resource, because the complete state can remain inside a fixed total-energy sector while a relative temporal mode remains statistically resolvable.

The paper establishes a two-regime answer. First, local Fisher information alone cannot obey a universal high-frequency energy bound under unrestricted state synthesis: at fixed mean energy, Fisher information can remain finite while the affine neighborhood over which its tangent is physical collapses. For nonzero physical tangent radius, arbitrary finite-copy collective measurements obey a two-sided spectral-survival law. At a rank-changing boundary, the relevant resource changes order: positive second-order endpoint synthesis is required. In an autonomous clock--signal exchange this yields sharp total synthesis-action coefficients of $\hbar\nu/4$ for bilateral synthesis and $\hbar\nu/2$ for one-sided synthesis. Fixed-total-energy constructions attain both constants even though global time-translation asymmetry and signed total-energy curvature vanish.

The result is extended in two directions intended to rule out natural loopholes rather than broaden the claim artificially. Arbitrary coherent baseline support is handled by a support/endpoint geometric reduction with a canonical domain/range endpoint-incidence action, and multiple exchange frequencies obey a single shared-Hessian spectral-action sum. A single Fourier measurement simultaneously saturates the complete frequency-weighted sum in a fixed-energy shell.

A separate companion manuscript addresses a natural dynamical question raised by the present kinematic framework. For a prescribed feasible rank-changing kernel-curvature contraction $C$, it derives the exact minimum state-weighted quadratic unitary-coupling cost $V_{\min}=\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}C$ and, in the clean autonomous single-gap specialization, $\mathcal A_{\rm ex}^{(2)}=\hbar\nu V_{\min}$. The present manuscript does not rely on that companion theorem or proof: its survival/synthesis results and sharp coefficients are self-contained. The companion result is cited only to show that the boundary action identified here also admits an exact minimum-coupling interpretation within a separately defined implementation problem.

We believe the manuscript is particularly suited to *PRX Quantum* through **exceptional connection and insight**. It links relational quantum time, quantum metrology, asymmetry/coherence resource theory, and rank-changing quantum-state geometry while identifying an operational resource distinction not captured by any one of those frameworks alone. The manuscript does not claim a new generic resource theory of time, a new Page--Wootters mechanism, or a universal implementation-energy law. It explicitly distinguishes the present state-family resource from shared asymmetry, quantum-Fisher energetic coherence, quantitative Wigner--Araki--Yanase/coherence-cost results, waveform Holevo bounds, and protocol-level energy-constrained metrology.

Detailed proofs, edge-case statements, and the exact common-record qutrit benchmark are provided in the Supplemental Material. The main text is designed to stand alone around the two-regime physical principle and its sharp autonomous constructions.
