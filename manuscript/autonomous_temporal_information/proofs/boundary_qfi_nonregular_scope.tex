\subsection{SLD-QFI corollary and nonregular boundary information}
\label{supp:qfi-nonregular}

The main boundary theorems are stated for the baseline score-Fisher block because that is the information carried by the first-order temporal tangent under one common measurement. The same synthesis action nevertheless controls the standard symmetric-logarithmic-derivative (SLD) quantum Fisher information of the pure-boundary tangent itself.

Let
\begin{equation}
PAP=0,\qquad QAQ=0,\qquad A=X+Y^\dagger,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
X=QAP,\qquad Y=QA^\dagger P,
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
J_+=\Tr(X\rho_0^+X^\dagger),\qquad
J_-=\Tr(Y\rho_0^+Y^\dagger).
\end{equation}
For the cosine and sine tangent operators
\begin{equation}
D_c=\frac{A+A^\dagger}{2},\qquad
D_s=\frac{A-A^\dagger}{2i},
\end{equation}
the support-to-kernel blocks are
\begin{equation}
K_c=\frac{X+Y}{2},\qquad
K_s=\frac{X-Y}{2i}.
\end{equation}
Using the standard finite-dimensional SLD-QFI formula in an eigenbasis of $\rho_0$ gives
\begin{equation}
H^{\rm SLD}_{cc}=4\Tr(K_c\rho_0^+K_c^\dagger),\qquad
H^{\rm SLD}_{ss}=4\Tr(K_s\rho_0^+K_s^\dagger).
\end{equation}
The weighted parallelogram identity therefore yields
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Tr H^{\rm SLD}=2(J_++J_-).
}
\label{eq:sld-trace-pure-boundary}
\end{equation}

In the clean autonomous exact-exchange geometry, each orientation creates one signal endpoint and one oppositely directed clock endpoint. The second-order positivity bounds used above imply
\begin{equation}
\Delta T_{S,+}+\Delta T_{S,-}\ge J_++J_-,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\Delta T_{C,+}+\Delta T_{C,-}\ge J_++J_-.
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
\Aact_C^{(2)}+\Aact_S^{(2)}
\ge\frac{\hbar\nu}{2}(J_++J_-)
=\frac{\hbar\nu}{4}\Tr H^{\rm SLD}.
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Aact_C^{(2)}+\Aact_S^{(2)}
\ge\frac{\hbar\nu}{4}\Tr H^{\rm SLD}.
}
\label{eq:sld-action-corollary}
\end{equation}
The coefficient is sharp in both fixed-shell constructions in the main text. For independent copies the SLD-QFI matrix is additive, so the same per-copy statement holds for every finite $N$.

The one-sided case also makes the distinction between quantum-statistical and common-record information explicit. There $J_-=0$, so $\Tr H^{\rm SLD}=2J_+$, whereas the sharp common-record score-Fisher trace is only $J_+$. The stronger one-sided common-record action coefficient therefore reflects simultaneous-measurement incompatibility between the cosine and sine quadratures, not absence of a quantum-statistical tangent bound.

The continuous Bures metric contains additional information that is not determined by the first derivative. At a rank-changing point, four times the Bures metric, denoted here by $H_c$, obeys \cite{Safranek2017}
\begin{equation}
H_c=H^{\rm SLD}+2\sum_{\lambda_k=0}\mathcal H_k,
\label{eq:safranek-bures}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal H_k$ is the parameter Hessian of a zero eigenvalue $\lambda_k$. The second term is genuine second-order path data.

This extra term cannot be bounded by a resource assigned only to the chosen first-order temporal mode. To see this, enlarge the Hilbert space by an empty spectator state $|r\rangle\in Q$ lying outside the temporal endpoint-incidence support, choose a normalized support vector $|p\rangle\in P$ with nonzero baseline population, and modify any local physical completion by
\begin{equation}
\widetilde\rho_\alpha(x,y)
=\rho(x,y)+\alpha(x^2+y^2)
\bigl(|r\rangle\!\langle r|-|p\rangle\!\langle p|\bigr),
\qquad \alpha>0.
\label{eq:spectator-curvature-family}
\end{equation}
For a sufficiently small parameter disk this remains a density operator. Its first derivatives at the origin are unchanged, so $A$, $F^{\rm tan}$, $J_\pm$, and the SLD-QFI tangent in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sld-trace-pure-boundary} are unchanged. If the temporal endpoint cost vanishes on $|r\rangle$, the mode-specific synthesis action is unchanged as well. However the spectator eigenvalue is
\begin{equation}
\lambda_r(x,y)=\alpha(x^2+y^2)+O(\|(x,y)\|^3),
\end{equation}
with Hessian $2\alpha I_2$. Equation~\eqref{eq:safranek-bures} therefore increases $\Tr H_c$ by $8\alpha$. Since $\alpha$ is arbitrary (with the local physical radius reduced accordingly), no finite bound on the full boundary Bures metric can follow from the first-order temporal tangent and a cost that prices only its endpoint sectors.

This spectator-curvature construction explains the scope of the main theorem. The score-Fisher block isolates information carried by the selected first-order temporal mode; the SLD-QFI corollary shows that support-to-kernel quantum tangent information is still action-controlled. A bound on the full continuous Bures metric requires charging all second-order parameter curvature, including curvature injected into sectors unrelated to that temporal mode.
