# PRX Quantum Popular Summary — R1

How can one part of a closed quantum system act as a clock for another part if the system as a whole does not change with time? Relational quantum clocks show that this is possible: a clock and signal can evolve relative to each other while their total state remains stationary. We ask what physical resource limits the timing information carried in this situation.

We find two regimes. Away from the boundary of quantum state space, high-frequency timing information must be supported by population already present in the energy sectors that participate in the clock--signal exchange. At a boundary, a smooth encoding can instead create previously absent population. The relevant resource then changes from pre-existing population to the rate at which new population is synthesized.

We derive measurement-independent bounds for both regimes, prove sharp examples with exactly fixed total energy, and obtain a multi-frequency sum rule. The results show that purely relational timing information remains physically constrained even when the complete system has no global time-translation asymmetry.
