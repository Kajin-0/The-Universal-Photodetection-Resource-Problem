\title{Two spectral-resource regimes for autonomous temporal information}

\author{Anonymous}
\affiliation{Anonymous}

\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Working theorem-first abstract.}
A local Fisher metric can remain finite at arbitrarily high Bohr frequency while the baseline mean energy is fixed, so temporal Fisher information alone cannot obey a universal energy--bandwidth law under unrestricted state synthesis.  We identify the missing physical structure and obtain two complementary resource regimes.  For a nonzero affine physical tangent radius, finite-copy classical Fisher information at an exact energy gap is bounded by pre-existing spectral survival; in a globally stationary clock--signal exchange the same information must be backed on both sides of the relational cut.  At a rank-changing boundary the affine radius can vanish while an exact nonlinear family remains informative.  There the resource moves to second order: baseline-empty endpoint population must be synthesized, opposite endpoint orientations combine by a sharp Minkowski law, and an autonomous exchange requires a positive two-sided synthesis action even inside a fixed-total-energy eigenspace with zero global time-translation asymmetry.  We extend the construction to arbitrary coherent support using shorted endpoint geometry and a canonical domain/range endpoint-incidence operator, and derive a frequency-weighted multi-gap action sum that is simultaneously saturated by one common Fourier measurement in a fixed-total-energy shell.  The synthesis action is a kinematic resource of the encoded state family, not a claim about total implementation energy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem, conventions, and claim boundary}
\label{sec:model}

We consider local two-parameter quantum statistical models whose two real coordinates are the cosine and sine quadratures of one temporal mode.  Let $A$ denote the corresponding complex tangent and define
\begin{equation}
D_c=\frac{A+A^\dagger}{2},\qquad
D_s=\frac{A-A^\dagger}{2i}.
\label{eq:quadratures}
\end{equation}
For a POVM $\{M_y\}$ at the baseline state $\rhozero$, write
\begin{equation}
p_y=\Tr(\rhozero M_y),\qquad z_y=\Tr(A M_y).
\end{equation}
The outcome derivatives are $\partial_c p_y=\operatorname{Re}z_y$ and $\partial_s p_y=\operatorname{Im}z_y$, so our two-quadrature classical Fisher trace is
\begin{equation}
\Tr \Fcl_1=\sum_{y:p_y>0}\frac{|z_y|^2}{p_y}.
\label{eq:FItrace}
\end{equation}
For $N$ independently encoded copies, $\Fcl_N$ denotes the Fisher matrix of an arbitrary joint POVM; no separability or asymptotic assumption is imposed unless stated explicitly.

An operator $A_\nu$ is an exact positive Bohr-gap tangent of frequency $\nu>0$ for Hamiltonian $H$ when
\begin{equation}
[H,A_\nu]=\hbar\nu A_\nu.
\label{eq:bohrgap}
\end{equation}
For an autonomous clock--signal pair the corresponding exact exchange condition is
\begin{equation}
[H_S,A_\nu]=+\hbar\nu A_\nu,
\qquad
[H_C,A_\nu]=-\hbar\nu A_\nu,
\label{eq:exchange}
\end{equation}
which implies $[H_C+H_S,A_\nu]=0$.

The resource statements below are deliberately distinct from general resource theories of time-translation asymmetry, the Page--Wootters mechanism, quantitative Wigner--Araki--Yanase constraints, waveform Holevo bounds, and protocol-level energy-constrained quantum metrology.  Those frameworks supply essential prior structure \cite{PageWootters1983,MarvianSpekkens2014Modes,CarmoSoaresPinto2021,TajimaShiraishiSaito2020,Marvian2022,YamaguchiTajima2023,GardnerEtAl2024,ChenYang2026}.  Our target is narrower: frequency-resolved necessary resources for a local \emph{relational} temporal Fisher mode, including rank-changing physical families for which global time-translation asymmetry can vanish identically.

\subsection{Physical affine tangent radius}

\begin{definition}[Linear physical tangent radius]
For a baseline $\rhozero$ and complex tangent $A$, $\Rlin(\rhozero,A)$ is the supremum of $R\ge0$ such that
\begin{equation}
\rhozero+xD_c+yD_s\succeq0
\quad\text{for every }x^2+y^2\le R^2.
\label{eq:RlinDef}
\end{equation}
If no nonzero disk is physical, $\Rlin=0$.
\end{definition}

This quantity is a property of the affine tangent, not the radius of an arbitrary nonlinear physical family.  A rank-deficient model can have $\Rlin=0$ while an exact nonlinear state family remains physical because second-order population is generated automatically.

\section{Fixed mean energy does not bound unrestricted local Fisher information}
\label{sec:nogo}

% SOURCE: WP02_LOCAL_FISHER_NO_GO_AND_ROBUST_TANGENT_RADIUS_LAW.md

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-energy high-frequency local-Fisher counterexample]
\label{prop:nogo}
Fix an excess mean energy $E>0$ and a constant $c>0$.  For every $\nu>E/\hbar$, let
\begin{equation}
H_\nu=\hbar\nu |1\rangle\!\langle1|,
\qquad
\rhozero=(1-p)|0\rangle\!\langle0|+p|1\rangle\!\langle1|,
\qquad
p=\frac{E}{\hbar\nu},
\end{equation}
and choose $A_\nu=2c|1\rangle\!\langle0|$.  Then $\Tr(\rhozero H_\nu)=E$ for all $\nu$, while an equatorial covariant qubit POVM has
\begin{equation}
\Tr \Fcl_1=4c^2
\end{equation}
independently of $\nu$.  The affine physical radius is instead
\begin{equation}
\Rlin^2=\frac{p(1-p)}{c^2}\xrightarrow[\nu\to\infty]{}0.
\end{equation}
Hence no baseline-energy-only law can force arbitrary local Fisher information to vanish at high frequency without an additional physical-robustness or finite-amplitude assumption.
\end{proposition}

The counterexample identifies the variable lost by a purely local metric: the state-space neighborhood over which the tangent itself remains physical.

\section{Finite-radius spectral-survival laws}
\label{sec:finite-radius}

Assume first that $\rhozero$ is stationary under $H$ and $A_\nu$ obeys Eq.~\eqref{eq:bohrgap}.  Let $P_{U,\nu}$ and $P_{D,\nu}$ be the upper- and lower-endpoint energy projectors supporting the range and domain of the $+\nu$ mode, and define
\begin{equation}
U_\nu=\Tr(\rhozero P_{U,\nu}),\qquad
D_\nu=\Tr(\rhozero P_{D,\nu}).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Robust Fisher--survival law]
\label{thm:single-survival}
If $\Rlin>0$, then for every finite $N$ and every joint POVM on $N$ independently encoded copies,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\Rlin^2}{4}\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N^{(\nu)}}{N}
\le \min\{D_\nu,U_\nu\}.
}
\label{eq:robust-survival}
\end{equation}
If $H$ is semibounded with lower edge $E_*$ and
\begin{equation}
T(\nu)=\Pr_{\rhozero}[H-E_*\ge\hbar\nu],
\end{equation}
then $U_\nu\le T(\nu)$ and therefore
\begin{equation}
\frac{\Rlin^2}{4}\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N^{(\nu)}}{N}\le T(\nu).
\label{eq:tail-survival}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for finite mean excess energy $\bar E^+=\Tr[\rhozero(H-E_*)]$,
\begin{equation}
\bar E^+\ge
\frac{\hbar\nu\Rlin^2}{4}\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N^{(\nu)}}{N}.
\label{eq:energy-corollary}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof uses the numerical-radius characterization
\begin{equation}
\Rlin^{-1}=w(\rhozero^{-1/2}A_\nu\rhozero^{-1/2})
\end{equation}
on the support, followed by $\|B\|\le2w(B)$ and an outcome-wise weighted Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz inequality.  The generic numerical-radius factor is asymptotically sharp in Proposition~\ref{prop:nogo}.  Numerical-radius mathematics itself is standard \cite{BhatiaJain2023}.

\subsection{Globally stationary relative time}

Let $C$ and $S$ have semibounded local Hamiltonians and suppose the baseline is globally stationary,
\begin{equation}
[\rhozero,H_C+H_S]=0,
\end{equation}
while $A_\nu$ obeys Eq.~\eqref{eq:exchange}.  Separate stationarity under $H_C$ and $H_S$ is not required for the support-sensitive extension; what matters is the exact local gap structure of the tangent and the physical tangent radius.

Define local upper tails from the local lower spectral edges $E_{C,*},E_{S,*}$,
\begin{equation}
T_X(\nu)=\Pr_{\rhozero}[H_X-E_{X,*}\ge\hbar\nu],\qquad X\in\{C,S\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Autonomous dual-survival law]
\label{thm:dual-survival}
For every finite $N$ and arbitrary joint POVM,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\Rlin^2}{4}\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N^{(\nu)}}{N}
\le \min\{T_C(\nu),T_S(\nu)\}.
}
\label{eq:dual-survival}
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\bar E_C^+ +\bar E_S^+
\ge
\frac{\hbar\nu\Rlin^2}{2}
\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N^{(\nu)}}{N}.
\label{eq:dual-energy}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Equation~\eqref{eq:dual-survival} is a relational statement: the global family can remain invariant under the total Hamiltonian while the local signal and clock exchange a matching gap.  The resource is then matched local spectral support rather than global time-translation asymmetry.

\section{Rank-changing boundary: second-order synthesis replaces survival}
\label{sec:boundary}

% SOURCES: WP07 and WP09.

Let $\rho(x,y)$ be a two-sided $C^2$ physical family with $\rho(0,0)=\rhozero$, let $P=\supp\rhozero$ and $Q=\id-P$, and let its first derivatives be Eq.~\eqref{eq:quadratures}.

\begin{theorem}[One-sided boundary synthesis]
\label{thm:one-sided-boundary}
Suppose $P_U P=0$ and
\begin{equation}
A=P_U A P.
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation}
J(A|\rhozero)=\Tr(A\rhozero^+A^\dagger),
\qquad
T_U(x,y)=\Tr[P_U\rho(x,y)].
\end{equation}
Then for every finite $N$ and arbitrary collective POVM,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N}{N}
\le J(A|\rhozero)
\le \Delta T_U(0),
}
\label{eq:one-sided-boundary}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta=\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2$.
\end{theorem}

The first inequality is an operational arbitrary-POVM score bound; the second is the second-order positive-semidefinite-cone constraint.  Rank-changing QFI/Bures geometry is established prior mathematics \cite{Safranek2017}; the present use is to identify the positive frequency-resolved endpoint population that must be synthesized.

For a pure boundary tangent with $PAP=QAQ=0$, write
\begin{equation}
X=QAP,\qquad Y=QA^\dagger P,\qquad A=X+Y^\dagger,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
J_+=\Tr(X\rhozero^+X^\dagger),\qquad
J_-=\Tr(Y\rhozero^+Y^\dagger).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Bilateral boundary Minkowski law]
\label{thm:bilateral-boundary}
For every finite $N$ and arbitrary collective POVM,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sqrt{\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N}{N}}
\le \sqrt{J_+}+\sqrt{J_-}.
}
\label{eq:boundary-minkowski}
\end{equation}
If $X$ and $Y$ enter mutually orthogonal baseline-empty endpoint sectors $P_+$ and $P_-$, and
\begin{equation}
T_\pm(x,y)=\Tr[P_\pm\rho(x,y)],
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N}{N}
\le
\left[\sqrt{\Delta T_+(0)}+\sqrt{\Delta T_-(0)}\right]^2.
}
\label{eq:boundary-curvature}
\end{equation}
The square-root composition is sharp; ordinary additive endpoint-curvature accounting is false in general.
\end{theorem}

An exact three-level positive-gap family with a Fourier measurement attains Eq.~\eqref{eq:boundary-curvature} and violates the naive additive proposal $\Tr F\le\Delta T_++\Delta T_-$ by exactly a factor of two.

\section{Autonomous zero-radius synthesis action}
\label{sec:autonomous-action}

% SOURCE: audited WP18.

Return to the exact exchange condition Eq.~\eqref{eq:exchange} and assume the pure-boundary case $PAP=0$.  In a clean spectral geometry, $X$ synthesizes a signal-upper and clock-lower endpoint, while $Y$ synthesizes a signal-lower and clock-upper endpoint.  Denote their local population Laplacians by
\begin{equation}
\Delta T_{S,+},\ \Delta T_{S,-},\ \Delta T_{C,+},\ \Delta T_{C,-}.
\end{equation}
Define the positive absolute-gap synthesis actions
\begin{equation}
\Aact_S^{(2)}=\frac{\hbar\nu}{4}(\Delta T_{S,+}+\Delta T_{S,-}),
\qquad
\Aact_C^{(2)}=\frac{\hbar\nu}{4}(\Delta T_{C,+}+\Delta T_{C,-}).
\label{eq:local-actions}
\end{equation}
They are positive endpoint-incidence resources; they are not signed subsystem-energy curvatures.

\begin{theorem}[Autonomous dual synthesis-action law]
\label{thm:dual-action}
For every finite $N$ and arbitrary collective POVM:

\emph{(i) Bilateral synthesis.} If both $X$ and $Y$ are present in clean baseline-empty endpoint sectors,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Aact_C^{(2)}+\Aact_S^{(2)}
\ge
\frac{\hbar\nu}{4}\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N}{N}.
}
\label{eq:dual-action-bilateral}
\end{equation}

\emph{(ii) One-sided synthesis.} If only one boundary orientation is present,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Aact_C^{(2)}+\Aact_S^{(2)}
\ge
\frac{\hbar\nu}{2}\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N}{N}.
}
\label{eq:dual-action-one-sided}
\end{equation}

Both coefficients are sharp already at one copy.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Fixed-total-energy sharpness}

Let
\begin{equation}
|L\rangle=|2_C,0_S\rangle,\quad
|M\rangle=|1_C,1_S\rangle,\quad
|U\rangle=|0_C,2_S\rangle,
\end{equation}
with equal local level spacing $\hbar\nu$, and take
\begin{equation}
\rhozero=|M\rangle\!\langle M|,
\qquad
A_\nu=c(|U\rangle\!\langle M|+|M\rangle\!\langle L|).
\end{equation}
The exactly normalized family
\begin{align}
|\psi(x,y)\rangle={}&
\sqrt{1-\frac{c^2}{2}(x^2+y^2)}\,|M\rangle
+\frac c2(x+i y)|L\rangle
+\frac c2(x-i y)|U\rangle
\label{eq:bilateral-extremizer}
\end{align}
is physical on the open disk $x^2+y^2<2/c^2$ and remains entirely inside the total-energy-$2\hbar\nu$ eigenspace.  Hence the global time-translation asymmetry and the signed total-energy curvature vanish identically.  Nevertheless each local endpoint Laplacian equals $c^2$, a three-outcome Fourier measurement gives $\Tr F_1=4c^2$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual-action-bilateral} is saturated exactly.

The one-sided coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:dual-action-one-sided} is likewise saturated by a two-state fixed-shell family.  With the convention in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quadratures}, its exact ket is proportional to $\sqrt{1-c^2(x^2+y^2)}|D\rangle+c(x-i y)|U\rangle$.

\section{Arbitrary coherent support: a mixed survival--synthesis bridge}
\label{sec:mixed}

% SOURCE: audited WP19, with WP11/WP13 machinery mostly delegated to supplement.

Let $P=\supp\rhozero$, $Q=\id-P$ and suppose $A_\nu$ obeys Eq.~\eqref{eq:exchange}.  First-order physicality gives $QA_\nu Q=0$.  Define
\begin{equation}
B=PA_\nu P,\qquad
K_+=QA_\nu P,\qquad
K_-=QA_\nu^\dagger P,
\label{eq:mixed-decomp}
\end{equation}
and weighted tangent operators
\begin{align}
Z_B^+&=B\rhozero^+B^\dagger,&
Z_B^-&=B^\dagger\rhozero^+B,\\
Z_+&=K_+\rhozero^+K_+^\dagger,&
Z_-&=K_-\rhozero^+K_-^\dagger.
\end{align}
Write $J_B^\pm=\Tr Z_B^\pm$ and $J_\pm=\Tr Z_\pm$.

The support-preserving term $B$ need not itself remain an exact Bohr-gap operator when $P$ fails to commute with the local Hamiltonians.  Its physical spectral resource therefore includes the principal-angle geometry between its information-bearing output subspace and the compressed energy endpoints.  Define $a_+$ and $a_-$ as the two-sided shorted endpoint-survival ceilings obtained from that geometry, so that
\begin{equation}
J_B^+\le a_+,
\qquad
J_B^-\le a_-.
\label{eq:a-ceilings}
\end{equation}
Explicit shorting formulas are given in the Supplemental Material; in the commuting clean limit they reduce to the WP02/WP03 survival resources.

For the boundary contribution define the canonical endpoint-role projectors of the \emph{full} exact exchange,
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}=\supp(A_\nu A_\nu^\dagger),
\qquad
\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}=\supp(A_\nu^\dagger A_\nu).
\label{eq:inout}
\end{equation}
Both commute with $H_C$ and $H_S$.  The canonical clock-plus-signal kernel endpoint-incidence cost is
\begin{equation}
G_{\mathrm{ex}}=2\hbar\nu\,Q(\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}+\Pi_{\mathrm{in}})Q.
\label{eq:Gex}
\end{equation}
For a $C^2$ family define
\begin{equation}
C_\Delta=Q(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2)\rho(0)Q,
\qquad
\Aact_{\mathrm{ex}}^{(2)}=\frac14\Tr(G_{\mathrm{ex}}C_\Delta).
\label{eq:Aex}
\end{equation}
Second-order positivity gives $C_\Delta\succeq Z_++Z_-$.  Let $R_\pm=\supp Z_\pm$ and
\begin{equation}
g_\pm=\lambda_{\min}\!\left(R_\pm G_{\mathrm{ex}}R_\pm\big|_{R_\pm}\right)
\label{eq:gpm}
\end{equation}
for nonzero components.  Then
\begin{equation}
g_+J_+ +g_-J_-\le4\Aact_{\mathrm{ex}}^{(2)}.
\label{eq:action-budget}
\end{equation}

For $a,e\ge0$ and $p,q>0$, define
\begin{equation}
\Psi_a(e;p,q)=
\begin{cases}
(\sqrt a+\sqrt{e/q})^2,&e\le ap^2/q,\\[1mm]
(e+pa)(p^{-1}+q^{-1}),&e\ge ap^2/q.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:Psi}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Noncommuting autonomous mixed resource law]
\label{thm:mixed}
For every finite $N$ and arbitrary collective POVM,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{\Tr \Fcl_N}{N}
\le
\min\!\left\{
\Psi_{a_+}(4\Aact_{\mathrm{ex}}^{(2)};g_+,g_-),
\Psi_{a_-}(4\Aact_{\mathrm{ex}}^{(2)};g_-,g_+)
\right\}.
}
\label{eq:mixed-master}
\end{equation}
The theorem reduces to dual pre-existing survival when $K_\pm=0$ and to the autonomous synthesis-action law when $B=0$ in the clean endpoint geometry.
\end{theorem}

The operator $G_{\mathrm{ex}}$ counts endpoint \emph{roles}.  If one energy state lies in both $\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}$ and $\Pi_{\mathrm{out}}$, that role is counted twice.  Equation~\eqref{eq:Aex} is a kinematic kernel-resolved endpoint-incidence action.  It is not generally the full Laplacian of a local endpoint population, a signed mean-energy curvature, or the total energy consumed by a physical implementation protocol.

A rank-two-support qutrit example gives an exact physical-resource ceiling $12$, while its SLD-QFI trace is $43/4$ and its exact one-copy common-record Fisher supremum is $55/8$.  This hierarchy illustrates that physical resource, quantum-statistical geometry, and measurement accessibility are distinct layers; the exact accessibility proof is deferred to the Supplemental Material.  Standard QFI and multiparameter compatibility theory provide the comparison layer \cite{BraunsteinCaves1994,GillMassar2000,LiEtAl2016,GardnerEtAl2024}.

\section{Multi-gap spectral-action sum}
\label{sec:multigap}

% SOURCE: audited WP20.

Consider one common $C^2$ multiparameter physical family with zero-radius pure-boundary exact exchange modes $A_k$ at positive gaps $\nu_k$.  For each mode define
\begin{equation}
X_k=QA_kP,
\qquad
Y_k=QA_k^\dagger P,
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
Z_{k,+}=X_k\rhozero^+X_k^\dagger,
\qquad
Z_{k,-}=Y_k\rhozero^+Y_k^\dagger.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation}
C_\Sigma=
Q\sum_k(\partial_{x_k}^2+\partial_{y_k}^2)\rho(0)Q.
\label{eq:Csigma}
\end{equation}
Summing the second-order PSD inequalities \emph{before} scalar endpoint reduction gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
C_\Sigma\succeq\sum_k(Z_{k,+}+Z_{k,-}).
}
\label{eq:Csigma-bound}
\end{equation}

Let $G\succeq0$ be one positive kernel spectral-cost operator and define
\begin{equation}
\Aact_{G,\Sigma}^{(2)}=\frac14\Tr(GC_\Sigma).
\end{equation}
For each nonzero orientation let $g_{k,\pm}$ be the minimum restriction of $G$ to $\supp Z_{k,\pm}$, and define the mode price
\begin{equation}
\gamma_k=
\begin{cases}
(g_{k,+}^{-1}+g_{k,-}^{-1})^{-1},&Z_{k,+},Z_{k,-}\ne0,\\
g_{k,+},&Z_{k,-}=0,\\
g_{k,-},&Z_{k,+}=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:gammak}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Multi-gap boundary action sum]
\label{thm:multigap}
For one fixed arbitrary joint POVM on $N$ independently encoded copies, let $\Fcl_{N,k}$ be the $2\times2$ Fisher block for coordinates $(x_k,y_k)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\sum_k\gamma_k\frac{\Tr \Fcl_{N,k}}{N}
\le4\Aact_{G,\Sigma}^{(2)}.
}
\label{eq:multigap-general}
\end{equation}
In a clean bilateral autonomous exchange geometry where $G$ assigns cost $2\hbar\nu_k$ to each orientation of mode $k$, $\gamma_k=\hbar\nu_k$ and
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Aact_{G,\Sigma}^{(2)}
\ge
\sum_k\frac{\hbar\nu_k}{4}
\frac{\Tr \Fcl_{N,k}}{N}.
}
\label{eq:multigap-clean}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Simultaneous sharpness with one common record}

Take a fixed-total-excitation shell
\begin{equation}
|n\rangle\equiv |m-n\rangle_C|m+n\rangle_S,
\qquad n=-m,\ldots,m,
\end{equation}
with baseline $|0\rangle$ and
\begin{equation}
A_k=c_k(|k\rangle\!\langle0|+|0\rangle\!\langle-k|),
\qquad \nu_k=k\omega_0.
\label{eq:star-modes}
\end{equation}
The exact common nonlinear family is
\begin{align}
|\psi(\bm x,\bm y)\rangle={}&
\sqrt{1-\frac12\sum_{k=1}^m c_k^2(x_k^2+y_k^2)}\,|0\rangle\nonumber\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^m\frac{c_k}{2}(x_k+i y_k)|-k\rangle
+\sum_{k=1}^m\frac{c_k}{2}(x_k-i y_k)|k\rangle.
\label{eq:star-family}
\end{align}
It is physical on the open ellipsoid $\frac12\sum_k c_k^2(x_k^2+y_k^2)<1$ and remains globally stationary throughout.

For $d=2m+1$, the discrete Fourier basis
\begin{equation}
|v_j\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt d}\sum_{n=-m}^{m}e^{in\phi_j}|n\rangle,
\qquad \phi_j=\frac{2\pi j}{d},
\end{equation}
gives the full common-record Fisher matrix
\begin{equation}
F_{x_kx_l}=2c_k^2\delta_{kl},\qquad
F_{y_ky_l}=2c_k^2\delta_{kl},\qquad
F_{x_ky_l}=0.
\label{eq:fourier-F}
\end{equation}
Thus $\Tr F_{1,k}=4c_k^2$ for every mode simultaneously.  With
\begin{equation}
G=2\hbar\omega_0\sum_{n\ne0}|n|\,|n\rangle\!\langle n|,
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:multigap-clean} is saturated term by term and in the complete weighted sum.

For overlapping mode ranges in a general model, we do not claim that a sum of independently chosen mode-labelled endpoint projectors defines a unique action.  The general theorem begins with one supplied positive operator $G$; target frequency prices then constitute an operator-design problem.  The simple frequency-diagonal interpretation above is sharp in the clean mode-separated geometry.

\section{Relation to prior work and scope}
\label{sec:prior}

The broad mathematical and conceptual ingredients of this work are established.  Page and Wootters showed how relational evolution can be represented within a stationary closed-system description \cite{PageWootters1983}.  Resource theories of asymmetry characterize harmonic modes and the coherence needed to break a symmetry \cite{MarvianSpekkens2014Modes}; shared asymmetry has been applied directly to the Page--Wootters mechanism \cite{CarmoSoaresPinto2021}.  Quantum Fisher information has an operational role as a resource of energetic coherence \cite{Marvian2022,YamaguchiTajima2023}, and conservation-law constraints on measurements and operations are treated by quantitative WAY and coherence-cost results \cite{MarvianSpekkensWAY2012,TajimaShiraishiSaito2020}.  Rank-changing QFI/Bures singularities are known \cite{Safranek2017}, while multiparameter and waveform-estimation theory separates SLD information from common-record attainability \cite{GillMassar2000,LiEtAl2016,GardnerEtAl2024}.  Energy-constrained metrology can also charge the total energy consumed by preparation, controls, and measurement \cite{ChenYang2026}.

The present resource is different in scope.  Equations~\eqref{eq:dual-survival} and \eqref{eq:dual-action-bilateral} concern a frequency-resolved relational mode that can be globally time-translation symmetric.  The finite-radius law charges pre-existing local spectral survival.  At a rank-changing boundary, the entropy-type shared-asymmetry resource is generally nonanalytic in amplitude, whereas the positive endpoint-incidence action supplies a finite quadratic necessary coefficient.  The action is kinematic: it is computed from the local Hessian of the encoded state family.  We do not claim that it equals the total physical energy required to synthesize that family dynamically.

Important open problems therefore remain outside the present theorem package: deriving a dynamical implementation cost that produces the kinematic action, extending the sharp spectral sum to controlled continua, and determining whether covariance-changing Gaussian families require an additional resource beyond endpoint curvature.

\section{Discussion and manuscript proof map}
\label{sec:discussion}

The theorem chain separates two mechanisms that a local Fisher metric alone conflates.  In the interior of state space, a temporal mode can be strong only over a finite affine neighborhood; multiplying Fisher information by that physical radius exposes a pre-existing spectral-survival cost.  At a rank-deficient boundary, the affine neighborhood can collapse even though a nonlinear family remains physical.  The missing population must then appear at second order, and its positive spectral incidence supplies the boundary resource.  In an autonomous exchange both mechanisms are two-sided because every signal energy step is matched by an opposite clock step.

The manuscript is intentionally organized around this finite-radius/boundary dichotomy rather than around the full operator-geometry development.  Detailed shorted-operator bounds, the shared-curvature allocation SDP, inverse-curvature principal angles, and exact common-record qutrit optimization are supplementary infrastructure.  Their role is to demonstrate that the main resource bridge survives coherent support and is not an artifact of diagonal baselines.
