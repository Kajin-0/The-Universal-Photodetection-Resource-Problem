# WP13 — Second-quantized scope and independent Poisson-event embedding

**Date:** 2026-08-21

## Status

**Physical-scope hardening of WP10/WP12.**

The quantum random-time mode theorem is not intrinsically a one-photon theorem. Its derivation depends only on decomposition into sectors of the **total generator of time translations**. Therefore fixed-photon-number multiphoton pulses, entangled states, internal degeneracy, and many-mode structure are already included when their total energy is used.

For an independent quantum-marked Poisson event source, the per-event mode budget extends additively to arbitrary mean event number. Subsequent formation of a common bosonic optical field, mode mixing, overlap, propagation, and detector processing are parameter-independent quantum channels and cannot increase the encoded QFI.

However, a phase-coherent indefinite-photon-number source is a genuinely different resource class: an optical carrier term proportional to number is not a global phase across different number sectors and cannot generally be subtracted. This distinction must be explicit in any manuscript.

---

## 1. WP10 is a total-energy-sector theorem

Let the physical Hilbert space be arbitrary, including a bosonic Fock space, and suppose the periodic time-translation generator has spectral decomposition

`H=E_* I + hbar*omega0 sum_{n>=0} n P_n`

on the participating invariant subspace.

The projectors `P_n` may have arbitrarily large or infinite degeneracy. For a pure state

`|Psi>=sum_n |Psi_n>`, `|Psi_n>=P_n|Psi>`,

define

`q_n=||Psi_n||^2`.

The WP10 random-time twirl and mode derivatives act only between total-energy sectors separated by `k hbar omega0`. The exact pure-state mode retention remains

`G_Q(k)=2 sum_n q_nq_{n+k}/(q_n+q_{n+k})`,

independent of how many photons, modes, particles, or entangled degrees of freedom are contained inside each `P_n`.

Thus all of the following are already included:

- fixed-`N` multiphoton states;
- frequency-entangled photon pairs;
- multimode fixed-number pulses;
- arbitrary degeneracy at fixed total energy;
- matter--light composite states, provided the relevant total generator is semibounded and admits the periodic sector structure.

The mean resource is the **total excess energy**

`Ebar^+=hbar*omega0 sum_n n q_n`.

---

## 2. Fixed photon number and optical carrier energy

For field modes near a carrier frequency `omega_c`, write schematically

`H = hbar omega_c N + H_env`,

where `H_env` contains detuning/envelope energy relative to the chosen carrier.

Inside an exact fixed-number sector `N=N0`, the carrier term is

`hbar omega_c N0 I`.

It therefore contributes only a global phase to a time-translated pure state and cancels from the density-operator orbit. The relevant energy resource may be shifted by this constant, exactly as in the gauge-invariant lower-edge construction of WP07.

Hence for fixed photon number it is correct that timing of the envelope is controlled by energy **above the participating lower edge**, not by an arbitrary common carrier offset.

---

## 3. Indefinite photon number changes the energy-reference physics

For an indefinite-number state, such as a phase-coherent optical coherent state,

`hbar omega_c N`

is **not** proportional to the identity. Different number sectors acquire different carrier phases under time translation.

If there is coherence between photon-number sectors and an appropriate phase reference, optical carrier energy can therefore contribute genuine time-translation asymmetry/information.

In particular, a coherent state with nonzero vacuum component typically has participating total-energy lower edge at the vacuum, so the relevant gauge-invariant excess energy includes the optical carrier contribution.

Therefore:

- do not subtract a carrier term `hbar omega_c N` as though it were a global energy zero for indefinite-`N` states;
- do not identify the fixed-`N` envelope-energy resource with the resource of a phase-coherent laser field;
- any broad universal field theorem must use the actual total time-translation generator and the lower edge of its participating support.

This resolves an apparent tension between envelope timing intuition and phase-sensitive coherent-state metrology.

---

## 4. Independent quantum-marked Poisson source

Consider a source in which latent events form a classical Poisson process. Each event carries an independent quantum excitation whose state is generated by the same random-time encoding model. Before the optical outputs of different source events are combined, represent the quantum source as a direct sum over event number:

`Rho_eps = directsum_{N>=0} P_mu(N) rho_eps^{tensor N}`,

where

`P_mu(N)=exp(-mu) mu^N/N!`

and the mean count `mu` is independent of the zero-mean temporal Fourier modulation parameter.

The orthogonal number-block weights are parameter independent. QFI therefore decomposes as

`F_Q[Rho_eps] = sum_N P_mu(N) F_Q[rho_eps^{tensor N})`.

Additivity of QFI for tensor-product copies gives

`F_Q[rho_eps^{tensor N}]=N F_Q[rho_eps]`.

Hence

`boxed: F_Q[Rho_eps]=mu F_Q[rho_eps]`.

The latent classical Poisson event-time experiment has FI

`F_in=mu/2`

per cosine/sine quadrature over the period. Therefore the source-normalized mode retention is exactly the same as the one-event result:

`boxed: G_Q,Poisson(k)=G_Q,single(k)`.

The mean total excess energy also scales by `mu`, so the energy-per-source-FI normalization remains unchanged.

---

## 5. From labeled source excitations to a common bosonic field

The direct-sum/tensor-product construction above may be regarded as a set of independent labeled source systems before emission into a common optical field.

Any physical emission/projection network that maps these labeled excitations into common bosonic field modes is a parameter-independent quantum process. This may include:

- symmetrization through the physical field degrees of freedom;
- linear or nonlinear mode mixing;
- temporal overlap of wavepackets;
- propagation through passive or active but parameter-independent optics;
- coupling into a detector with arbitrary coherent quantum memory.

Quantum Fisher information cannot increase under this parameter-independent processing.

Therefore **bosonic overlap after independent event encoding cannot invalidate the upstream WP10/WP12 mode budget.**

This gives a clean operational route from the quantum-marked Poisson source model to realistic common-field detection without requiring the photons to remain distinguishable at the detector.

The exact source-generation channel must be specified if a publication claims this mapping for a particular optical source, but the information-theoretic direction of the bound is robust.

---

## 6. What this does not cover

WP13 does not prove that every optical field with Poisson photocount statistics can be represented by the independent quantum-marked source model.

In particular:

- a pure coherent state has Poisson number statistics but also definite optical phase coherence;
- squeezed/coherent superpositions can carry temporal information not present in a classical mixture of independent event marks;
- strongly correlated multiphoton emission may encode the waveform collectively rather than as independent random event times.

Thus **Poisson counting statistics alone do not specify the quantum source state**.

Paper 2 intentionally starts from a classical Poisson direct-detection source model. WP13 supplies a quantum realization class consistent with independent quantum events, not a theorem about all quantum states that happen to yield Poisson counts under one measurement.

---

## 7. Fixed-N entanglement cannot evade the total-energy budget

Because the pure-state WP10 formula depends only on total-energy-sector probabilities `q_n`, entanglement or complicated multimode structure **within** each total-energy sector cannot improve the QFI mode budget at fixed `q_n`.

Entanglement can change the achievable total-energy distribution, but once that distribution and its mean are fixed, the WP10/WP12 upper bound remains.

This is useful conceptually: the random-time mode budget is controlled by the distribution of the generator of time translations, not by particle labels.

---

## 8. Relation to the continuous WP12 theorem

For a fixed-`N` multiphoton pulse with continuous total excess-energy density `q(w)`, the controlled periodic-to-continuum construction of WP12 applies to the **total** excitation frequency `w`.

Hence the measurement-independent continuum area theorem becomes

`boxed: int_R G_Q(nu)dnu <= pi Ebar_total^+/hbar`.

Any subsequent quantum detector process inherits this bound.

For an independent Poisson number of such pulses/events, both total QFI and total energy are additive, so the same normalized area law survives.

---

## 9. New high-value boundary: coherent-state intensity modulation

A distinct unresolved case is a phase-coherent indefinite-photon-number optical field whose **field amplitude/intensity waveform itself** is the encoded parameter, rather than a classical mixture over random event centers.

For a coherent state, the ultimate waveform QFI can equal the ideal Poisson direct-detection FI for suitable amplitude perturbations. Positivity of optical frequency still constrains arbitrarily rapid physical modulation, but the relevant resource necessarily includes the carrier/number-coherence contribution of the full Hamiltonian.

This problem connects WP12 to actual laser/coherent-state communication and could either:

1. produce a broader total-energy waveform-area law; or
2. furnish a counterexample showing that random-event encoding is essential to the current `pi` constant.

It is now the most important second-quantized source-class gate.

---

## 10. Decision

The bosonic-field concern does **not** invalidate WP10/WP12.

Current justified scope:

- arbitrary pure/mixed quantum pulse with semibounded total energy and random global event-time encoding;
- arbitrary fixed-photon-number multiphoton/entangled pulse;
- independent quantum-marked Poisson stream of such events;
- arbitrary subsequent quantum field formation, overlap, coherent detector memory, and measurement.

Still unresolved:

- phase-coherent indefinite-number optical sources with direct waveform encoding;
- general correlated many-body source processes where temporal modulation is not mediated by independent random event centers.

The next gate should analyze coherent-state waveform encoding using the full positive-frequency field Hamiltonian and compare its optimal temporal Fisher spectrum against the WP12 random-event bound.
