# WP06 — Positive-energy temporal Fisher spectral-area law

**Date:** 2026-08-21

## Status

**Strong theorem candidate; not yet priority-certified.**

This is the strongest surviving result in the grand-challenge program so far. It is qualitatively different from the discarded dissipation/QFI-only routes: positivity of energy places a sharp upper bound on the **integrated temporal Fisher-transfer spectrum** of a covariant event-timing readout.

The sharp functional-analytic inequality is classical and must be credited. The possible contribution is its exact photodetection/information-transfer interpretation, efficiency bookkeeping, and inverse Planck-scale bandwidth law.

---

## 1. Physical setup

Let a quantum excitation have Hamiltonian `H` with lower spectral edge `E0`, and define the nonnegative angular-frequency generator

`Omega = (H-E0)/hbar >= 0`.

Let the unknown arrival-time shift `theta` act covariantly,

`rho_theta = exp(-i Omega theta) rho exp(+i Omega theta)`.

Let the accessible primary event record be generated by a fixed time-covariant sub-POVM. Start with one unresolved click channel plus a no-click outcome. Let

`Q = int_R M(dt)`

be the total click effect. Covariance implies `[Q,Omega]=0`, and `0<=Q<=I`.

For state `rho`, define

`eta = Tr[rho Q]`

and the detected excitation-frequency moment

`omega_det = Tr[rho Q Omega]`.

In energy units,

`E_det = hbar * omega_det = Tr[rho Q(H-E0)]`.

Clearly

`0 <= E_det <= E_exc := Tr[rho(H-E0)]`.

Let the click-time subdensity be `p(t)` with

`int p(t)dt = eta`,

and conditional click density

`f(t)=p(t)/eta` when `eta>0`.

For the corresponding classical independent-event timing channel,

`G(nu) = eta |F(nu)|^2`,

where

`F(nu)=int exp(-i nu t) f(t)dt`.

Here `nu` is the **modulation angular frequency** of the weak source waveform, distinct from the positive excitation frequency variable carried by `Omega`.

## 2. Covariant POVM amplitude representation

By the generalized imprimitivity/Naimark theory of covariant POVMs, a time-covariant event POVM can be represented by a contraction/intertwiner from the physical positive-energy representation into a translation representation on

`L2(R_t; K)`

for a multiplicity/dilation Hilbert space `K`.

For a pure input state this gives a vector-valued timing amplitude

`u(t) in K`

such that

`p(t)=||u(t)||_K^2`.

Because the physical generator satisfies `Omega>=0` and the intertwiner preserves the time-translation representation, the Fourier transform `u_hat(w)` has support on `w>=0`.

Moreover

`||u||_2^2 = eta`

and its positive-frequency first moment is

`int_0^infinity w ||u_hat(w)||_K^2 dw = omega_det`

under a consistent unitary Fourier convention.

For mixed input states, purify `rho` and include the purifying space in `K`; the same representation and moment identities apply.

This is the key physical step that converts semibounded quantum energy into a Hardy-space regularity condition on a covariant timestamp amplitude.

---

## 3. Sharp vector-valued Hardy Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality

Pocovnicu (Analysis & PDE 4, 379--404, 2011; arXiv:1001.4037), Proposition 1.5, proves for scalar `v in H_+^{1/2}(R)`:

`||v||_4^4 <= (1/pi) ||v||_2^2 ||v||_{Hdot_+^{1/2}}^2`,

with equality iff

`v(t)=C/(t-z)`, `Im z<0`.

### Vector-valued extension with the same constant

Let `{e_j}` be an orthonormal basis of `K` and write

`u(t)=sum_j u_j(t)e_j`,

where every component `u_j` lies in the positive-frequency Hardy space.

Then

`int ||u(t)||^4 dt = sum_{j,k} int |u_j(t)|^2 |u_k(t)|^2 dt`.

By ordinary Cauchy--Schwarz in `L2(dt)`,

`int |u_j|^2 |u_k|^2 <= ||u_j||_4^2 ||u_k||_4^2`.

Applying Pocovnicu's sharp scalar inequality componentwise,

`||u_j||_4^2 <= pi^{-1/2} (M_j E_j)^{1/2}`,

where

`M_j=||u_j||_2^2`,

`E_j=||u_j||_{Hdot_+^{1/2}}^2`.

Hence

`int ||u||^4 <= (1/pi) [sum_j sqrt(M_j E_j)]^2`.

A second Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives

`[sum_j sqrt(M_j E_j)]^2 <= (sum_j M_j)(sum_j E_j)`.

Therefore

`boxed: int_R ||u(t)||^4 dt <= (1/pi) ||u||_2^2 ||u||_{Hdot_+^{1/2}(K)}^2`.

Passing from finite to countable expansions follows by monotone/`L2` approximation. Thus the sharp scalar constant survives arbitrary separable multiplicity.

Equality requires, in particular, equality in both Cauchy--Schwarz steps; this forces the nonzero components to be mutually proportional and to share the same scalar Hardy extremal. Hence equality is effectively rank one in multiplicity space, with common one-pole profile.

## 4. Main theorem — detected-energy spectral-area bound

Since `p(t)=||u(t)||^2`, the vector Hardy inequality gives

`int p(t)^2 dt <= eta * omega_det/pi`.

For `f=p/eta`,

`eta int f(t)^2 dt = (1/eta) int p(t)^2 dt <= omega_det/pi`.

Parseval for the classical timing-transfer spectrum gives

`int_{-infinity}^{infinity} G(nu)dnu = 2*pi*eta*int f(t)^2dt`.

Therefore

`boxed: int G(nu)dnu <= 2 omega_det`.

Equivalently, in energy units,

`boxed: int G(nu)dnu <= (2/hbar) E_det`.

Since `E_det<=E_exc`, a detector-independent source bound follows:

`boxed: int G(nu)dnu <= (2/hbar) E_exc`.

This is the proposed **positive-energy temporal Fisher spectral-area law**.

---

## 5. Fisher-equivalent bandwidth form

For one unresolved conditional timing density, Paper-1 notation gives

`B_FI = (1/2) int f(t)^2dt`

and

`int G(nu)dnu = 4*pi*eta*B_FI`.

Thus the main theorem is equivalent to

`boxed: eta B_FI <= E_det/h`.

Because `E_det<=E_exc`,

`boxed: eta B_FI <= E_exc/h`.

### Energy-independent efficiency

If the click effect is energy independent,

`Q=eta I`,

then

`E_det=eta E_exc`.

The efficiency cancels from the conditional timing-bandwidth bound:

`boxed: B_FI <= E_exc/h`.

Meanwhile the total transferred spectral area still scales with efficiency:

`int G <= 2 eta E_exc/hbar`.

This is physically sensible: uniform loss lowers how often an event is available but need not broaden the conditional timing distribution of detected events.

## 6. Flat-band inverse resource law

Suppose the source-normalized retention obeys

`G(2*pi*f) >= q`

for every ordinary frequency `|f|<=B`.

Then

`int G(nu)dnu >= 4*pi B q`.

Combining with the positive-energy area theorem yields

`4*pi Bq <= 2 E_det/hbar`,

hence

`boxed: E_det >= h B q`.

Since `E_exc>=E_det`, universally

`boxed: E_exc >= h B q`.

For energy-independent efficiency `Q=eta I`,

`boxed: E_exc >= h B (q/eta)`.

The ratio `q/eta` is the retained fraction relative to the detector's zero-frequency event efficiency.

This is an especially compact Planck-scale inverse law: maintaining a finite source-normalized information fraction over a temporal band requires a corresponding positive-energy scale.

## 7. Sharpness and extremal family

For a scalar ideal covariant timestamp (`eta=1`), Pocovnicu's equality family gives the normalized timing amplitude

`u_a(t) = sqrt(a/pi)/(t+i a)`

up to irrelevant phase/translation conventions.

The timestamp density is the Cauchy law

`f_a(t)=a/[pi(t^2+a^2)]`.

Its characteristic function is

`F_a(nu)=exp(-a |nu|)`

and therefore

`G_a(nu)=exp(-2a|nu|)`.

Directly,

`int G_a(nu)dnu = 1/a`.

The positive-frequency amplitude has exponential spectral intensity and mean excitation angular frequency

`omega_bar = 1/(2a)`.

Hence

`int G_a dnu = 2 omega_bar`,

so the area theorem is exactly saturated.

Also

`int f_a^2dt = 1/(2*pi*a)`,

`B_FI=1/(4*pi*a)=E_exc/h`.

Thus the conditional timing-bandwidth inequality is sharp.

For constant energy-independent loss `Q=eta I`, the same conditional Cauchy timing law gives

`G_a(nu)=eta exp(-2a|nu|)`

and saturates the stronger detected-energy form

`int G=2 E_det/hbar=2 eta E_exc/hbar`.

## 8. Accessible finite/countable marks

For a finite or countable accessible mark set, let mark `m` have subdensity

`p_m(t)=kappa_m f_m(t)`,

mass `kappa_m`, and detected excitation-frequency moment `omega_m`.

Applying the scalar/vector Hardy bound to each orthogonal mark channel gives

`int p_m^2dt <= kappa_m omega_m/pi`.

The complete marked-event Fisher spectral area is

`int G(nu)dnu = 2*pi sum_m kappa_m int f_m^2dt`

`=2*pi sum_m [1/kappa_m] int p_m^2dt`

`<=2 sum_m omega_m`.

The sum of the marked detected frequency moments is the total detected moment `omega_det`, so

`boxed: int G <= 2 omega_det`

again.

Thus resolving accessible marks does not evade the positive-energy area law.

A fully general measurable-mark version should be written using direct integrals/Radon--Nikodym disintegration, but no conceptual obstruction is presently visible.

## 9. Relation to WP02/WP03 QFI bound

WP03 gave

`B_FI <= sqrt(F_Q)/(4 sqrt(3)) <= Delta H/(2 sqrt(3) hbar)`.

WP06 is independent and often more physical:

`B_FI <= E_exc/h`

for an ideal/energy-independent-efficiency covariant timestamp.

The QFI/variance bound can be stronger for narrow energy distributions with large mean, while the positive-energy bound remains finite and informative for states with infinite energy variance but finite mean energy.

The two resource laws therefore constrain different aspects of the quantum timing state.

## 10. Relation to known time--energy and Renyi results

### Established ingredients

- Covariant time POVMs for semibounded Hamiltonians are classical quantum-measurement theory; see Holevo/Werner/generalized imprimitivity and Skulimowski, *Physics Letters A* 297, 129--136 (2002), DOI `10.1016/S0375-9601(02)00298-0`.
- Positive-energy time amplitudes naturally live in Hardy spaces through Naimark dilation.
- Pocovnicu's sharp positive-frequency Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality is established mathematics: *Analysis & PDE* 4, 379--404 (2011), DOI `10.2140/apde.2011.4.379`.
- Number/phase and energy/time Renyi uncertainty relations, strong Heisenberg limits, and average-energy metrology bounds are well developed; e.g. Hall, *Entropy* 24, 1679 (2022), arXiv:2210.14613.
- Time-delay QFI bounds in terms of spectral variance and broadband integrated-QFI bounds also exist.

### Targeted collision-search result so far

The searches performed to date have **not located the exact event-channel statements**

`eta B_FI <= E_det/h`

or

`int G(nu)dnu <= 2 E_det/hbar`,

nor the flat-retention corollary

`E_det >= h B q`

expressed as a source-to-record Fisher-information-transfer resource law.

This absence is **not priority certification**. The result may exist under an entropic/Hardy/time-observable formulation not yet located.

The paper must not claim novelty for the Hardy inequality or for covariant time POVMs themselves.

## 11. Physical interpretation

This theorem says something more specific than a conventional time--energy uncertainty relation.

The object being bounded is not a single timing variance or estimator RMSE. It is the **integrated source-normalized Fisher transfer over all temporal modulation frequencies** of the event channel.

A positive-energy excitation cannot support arbitrarily large total temporal-information transfer area.

In particular, idealized classical delta-time events correspond to infinite Fisher spectral area and therefore cannot arise as finite-mean-positive-energy covariant quantum timestamps under this model.

This supplies a possible quantum regularization of the ideal event-time abstraction used in classical photodetection theory.

Careful wording is mandatory: Paper 2's deterministic conditional delay atom is relative to a latent classical event time. WP06 does not directly invalidate that model. It identifies the finite-energy resource needed when the event time itself is represented as a physical covariant quantum timing degree of freedom.

---

## 12. Why this is potentially important

The sharp inverse law

`E_det >= h B q`

has the structure of a temporal information analogue of a quantum resource bound:

- `B` = temporal modulation band;
- `q` = guaranteed source-normalized Fisher retention over that band;
- `E_det` = detected positive excitation energy resource.

Unlike the failed dissipation conjectures, this bound follows from a genuinely universal quantum kinematic fact: semibounded energy forces analyticity/Hardy structure in time.

The extremal Cauchy/exponential family gives an exact equality case rather than merely an order-of-magnitude scaling.

This is the first theorem in the grand-challenge branch that currently appears capable of supporting a distinct paper if novelty survives.

It is **not** yet appropriate to describe it as Nobel-level.

## 13. Remaining hostile gates

1. **Covariant POVM rigor:** write the intertwiner/direct-integral theorem with precise hypotheses on the absolutely continuous positive-energy spectrum and multiplicity.
2. **Mixed states:** formalize the purification argument and confirm the detected energy moment is exactly `Tr[rho Q Omega]` under the dilation.
3. **General marks:** replace the countable-mark argument by a direct-integral/disintegration proof.
4. **Spectral thresholds:** if the physical spectrum starts at `E0>0`, confirm the relevant resource is excitation energy above the lower edge, not absolute rest/carrier energy, and state when optical carrier energy should or should not be subtracted.
5. **Photonic interpretation:** distinguish optical carrier energy from baseband sideband/temporal-envelope generator; determine which Hamiltonian actually generates the unknown arrival-time shift for a photon wavepacket.
6. **Prior art:** search time-energy Renyi/collision-entropy literature specifically for the `L2` time-density/mean-positive-energy inequality.
7. **Sharpness:** verify the Cauchy time law is physically attainable by a normalized positive-energy state and a standard covariant time POVM; characterize equality with multiplicity/marks.
8. **End-to-end mapping:** explicitly derive the Paper-1 event Fisher spectrum from the quantum timestamp distribution with no hidden normalization mismatch.
9. **Memory:** investigate whether an analogue survives arbitrary memory-bearing trajectory channels or whether WP06 is intrinsically a one-event theorem.

Do not draft a paper until gates 1--8 are closed.
