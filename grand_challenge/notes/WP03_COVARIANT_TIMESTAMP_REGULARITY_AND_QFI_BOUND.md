# WP03 — Covariant timestamp regularity and finite-QFI bandwidth theorem

**Date:** 2026-08-21

## Status

Proof repair / strengthening of WP02. This is a theorem candidate with classical ingredients. It corrects an over-strong interpretation in WP02: finite QFI is not needed merely to exclude literal moving timestamp atoms.

## 1. Setup

Let `rho_theta` be a family of normal quantum states on a Hilbert space and let `M(dt)` be a fixed POVM on the real timestamp line. Define the output probability measure

`P_theta(A)=Tr[rho_theta M(A)]`.

Assume the output experiment is covariant under the unknown time shift:

`P_theta(A)=P_0(A-theta)`.

The physically important unitary case is

`rho_theta = U_theta rho_0 U_theta^*`,

with `U_theta=exp(-i H theta/hbar)` strongly continuous.

## 2. Theorem A — quantum continuity forces an ordinary timing density

### Statement

If `theta -> rho_theta` is continuous in trace norm and the fixed POVM output is a translated probability law as above, then `P_0` is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure:

`P_0(dt)=f(t) dt`

for some `f in L1(R)`, `f>=0`, `int f=1`.

Consequently an exactly translated positive Dirac timing mass, or any purely singular translated timing law, cannot occur as the outcome law of such a continuously varying normal-state/fixed-measurement experiment.

### Proof

Measurement is contractive from trace distance to classical total variation:

`||P_theta-P_0||_TV <= (1/2)||rho_theta-rho_0||_1 -> 0`.

Covariance gives

`P_theta = T_theta P_0`,

so the finite measure `P_0` has norm-continuous translations.

For finite Borel measures on the locally compact group `R`, norm-continuity under translations is equivalent to absolute continuity with respect to Haar/Lebesgue measure. This is a classical theorem in harmonic analysis / translation-continuity theory.

Therefore `P_0=f dt`.

### Unitary normal-state corollary

For any strongly continuous unitary group `U_theta` and trace-class density operator `rho`, the orbit

`rho_theta=U_theta rho U_theta^*`

is trace-norm continuous. Hence every fixed covariant timestamp POVM applied to a normal state has an absolutely continuous output time distribution.

This uses no finite energy variance and no finite QFI.

## 3. Theorem B — finite QFI upgrades the density to finite shift Fisher information

Assume in addition that the quantum state family is differentiable in the Bures/DQM sense with finite quantum Fisher information `F_Q` at the operating point.

Any parameter-independent POVM is a quantum-to-classical channel, so the classical output experiment is Hellinger/DQM differentiable and obeys the Braunstein--Caves information bound

`I_shift <= F_Q`.

For the covariant location family `f(t-theta)`, DQM implies the square-root density has an `L2` weak derivative:

`sqrt(f) in H1(R)`,

and

`I_shift = 4 int |(sqrt(f))'|^2 dt`.

Thus finite QFI supplies a quantitative Sobolev timing regularity beyond Theorem A's absolute continuity.

## 4. Sharp timing-bandwidth consequence

The sharp one-dimensional Gagliardo--Nirenberg inequality gives

`int f^2 dt <= sqrt(I_shift)/(2 sqrt(3))`.

For the normalized one-event timing channel,

`B_FI = (1/2) int f^2 dt`,

so

`boxed: B_FI <= sqrt(I_shift)/(4 sqrt(3)) <= sqrt(F_Q)/(4 sqrt(3))`.

For a unitary time shift generated by `H`,

`F_Q <= 4 (Delta H)^2/hbar^2`,

therefore

`boxed: B_FI <= Delta H/(2 sqrt(3) hbar)`.

The classical first inequality is sharp, with equality for

`f(t)=(a/2) sech^2[a(t-t0)]`.

Quantum equality additionally requires a measurement saturating the QFI and a physically allowed state/POVM realizing the extremal timing law; this is a separate attainability question.

## 5. Crucial correction relative to WP02

Do **not** say:

> finite QFI is what forbids a moving timestamp atom.

The stronger truth is:

> trace-norm continuity of a normal quantum state family plus a fixed covariant measurement already forbids a singular translated timestamp law.

Finite QFI is needed for the **quantitative Fisher/Sobolev bandwidth bound**, not for mere absolute continuity.

## 6. Crucial distinction from Paper 2

Paper 2's ideal deterministic-delay atom is an atom of a **conditional delay kernel relative to a latent classical incident-event time**, not necessarily a Dirac atom in the unconditional quantum measurement outcome distribution for an unknown time-shift parameter.

Therefore Theorem A does not invalidate Paper 2's classical point-process model and must not be advertised as doing so.

A quantum/source-level model is required before connecting Paper 2's `1/e` high-frequency plateau to source energy bandwidth or QFI.

## 7. Prior art

Established ingredients include:

- norm-continuous translations of finite measures iff absolute continuity with respect to Haar measure (classical group/harmonic-analysis result; also treated in translation-continuity probability literature);
- covariant time POVMs / generalized systems of imprimitivity;
- Hellinger differentiability of regular location families and shift Fisher information;
- Braunstein--Caves QFI >= measurement CFI;
- QFI as a time-translation asymmetry resource;
- sharp Gagliardo--Nirenberg / Fisher--Renyi inequalities.

The possible contribution is the event-timing-channel synthesis and the exact `B_FI` bridge, not any individual ingredient.

## 8. Next decisive gate

The next question is whether this single-timestamp theorem can be lifted to the **trajectory-level Fisher spectrum** of a memory-bearing measurement apparatus.

Specifically, seek a physically meaningful quantum functional `Q(omega)` or integrated QFI rate such that the accessible classical trajectory spectrum satisfies an operator/spectral domination stronger than ordinary scalar QFI:

`G_accessible(omega) <= Q_resource(omega)`

or an integrated bound on `int G(omega)domega`.

A trivial restatement of quantum data processing is insufficient; the target must reveal a temporal resource or regularity not already contained in standard QFI theory.
