# PRX Quantum Popular Summary Draft

**Target length:** about 150 words

**No equations. Minimal jargon.**

Many sensors are described by a response time or bandwidth, but those quantities do not directly say how much information about a changing signal can reach a measurement record. We study a quantum setting in which a fixed excitation is emitted at a random time and the signal is encoded in the statistics of that emission time. We show that spectral population above the relevant energy-frequency gap places a ceiling on the temporal information recoverable by any downstream measurement, even with collective measurements on multiple excitations. Importantly, this limit is not tied to an ideal equally spaced spectrum: it extends to arbitrary semibounded discrete energy spectra at their actual Bohr gaps. For one fixed single-excitation measurement, retention at different frequencies is also globally constrained, making nearly perfect retention require a sharply diverging excess-energy resource. We further classify exactly which contiguous spectra saturate the one-copy ceiling. The result connects quantum metrology, symmetry, time-frequency optics, and photodetection through one operational spectral resource structure.
