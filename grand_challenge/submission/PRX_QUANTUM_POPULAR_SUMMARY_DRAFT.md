# PRX Quantum Popular Summary Draft

**Target length:** about 150 words

**No equations. Minimal jargon.**

Many sensors are described by a response time or bandwidth, but those quantities do not directly say how much information about a changing signal can actually reach a measurement record. We study a quantum version of this problem in which identical excitations are emitted at random times and the signal is encoded in the statistics of those emission times. We derive a sharp limit connecting the temporal information recoverable by any downstream measurement to the excitation's energy distribution. The result remains valid even when several excitations are measured jointly and when the emitted field undergoes arbitrary parameter-independent propagation, overlap, memory, and detector processing. We also identify an excitation family and measurement that exactly attain the limit. The theorem provides a direct bridge between quantum metrology, time-frequency information, and photodetection, while also showing why a different resource accounting is required when a waveform is actively encoded into the quantum state itself.
