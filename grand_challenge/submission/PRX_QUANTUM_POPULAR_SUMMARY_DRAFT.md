# PRX Quantum Popular Summary Draft

**Target length:** about 150 words

**No equations. Minimal jargon.**

Many sensors are described by a response time or bandwidth, but those quantities do not directly say how much information about a changing signal can reach a measurement record. We study a quantum setting in which a fixed excitation is emitted at a random time and the signal is encoded in the statistics of that emission time. We show that spectral population above a frequency gap places a sharp ceiling on the temporal information recoverable by any downstream measurement, even with collective measurements on multiple excitations. For one fixed single-excitation measurement, the retained information at different temporal frequencies is also constrained globally rather than independently. This makes nearly perfect retention increasingly expensive: the required excess energy diverges with a sharp inverse-square-root law. We further classify exactly which spectra saturate the one-copy ceiling, connecting the quantum problem to classical moment theory. The result links quantum metrology, symmetry, time-frequency optics, and photodetection through a common operational resource structure.
