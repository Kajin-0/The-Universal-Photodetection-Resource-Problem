# PRX Quantum Popular Summary Draft

**Target length:** about 150 words

**No equations. Minimal jargon.**

Many sensors are described by a response time or bandwidth, but those quantities do not directly say how much information about a changing signal can actually reach a measurement record. We study a quantum setting in which a fixed excitation is emitted at a random time and the signal is encoded in the statistics of that emission time. We show that the temporal information recoverable by any downstream measurement is limited by the fraction of the excitation's spectrum that survives above the corresponding frequency gap. The relevant energy is excitation energy above the active lower spectral edge, not an irrelevant common carrier offset. The limit survives collective measurements on multiple excitations and arbitrary parameter-independent field formation and detector processing. It is exactly attainable by one family of states and measurements, and a realistic transform-limited single-photon spectrum also approaches the ceiling closely. The result connects quantum metrology, symmetry, time-frequency quantum optics, and photodetection through one operational resource law.
