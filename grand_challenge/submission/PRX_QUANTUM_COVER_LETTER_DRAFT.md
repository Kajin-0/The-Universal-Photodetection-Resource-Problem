# PRX Quantum Cover Letter Draft

**Manuscript:** *Spectral Resource Laws for Temporal Fisher Information*

**Article type:** Research Article

Dear Editors of PRX Quantum,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Spectral Resource Laws for Temporal Fisher Information,”** for publication as a Research Article in PRX Quantum.

The manuscript asks an operational quantum-metrology question: when temporal structure is encoded in the probability distribution of the random emission time of a fixed quantum excitation, what spectral resource constrains the Fisher information that can reach an actual downstream measurement record?

The first result is a finite-copy arbitrary-measurement theorem. In an exact periodic formulation, for any finite number of independently encoded excitations and **any joint POVM**, including arbitrary entangled collective measurements, the accessible two-quadrature Fisher information in temporal harmonic `k` is bounded by paired generator-sector populations and, universally, by the excitation-population tail above the corresponding semibounded gap.

A central strengthening in the present manuscript shows that this mechanism is **not an artifact of an equally spaced Hamiltonian**. For an arbitrary semibounded pure-point Hamiltonian, with no global commensurability assumption, long-window random-time averaging isolates exact Bohr gaps. At any requested gap `nu`, the tangent retains the same partial-isometry factorization and every finite-copy joint POVM obeys

`Tr F_N^(nu)/N <= min(D_nu,U_nu) <= Pr(Omega >= nu)`.

Thus the population-tail Fisher ceiling is a Bohr-gap resource statement for general pure-point spectra. If the Hamiltonian contains no exact pair separated by `hbar nu`, the limiting local Fisher response at that gap vanishes.

Controlled periodic-to-continuum limits give the same survival law

`R(nu) <= Pr(Omega >= nu)`.

Here `Omega` is excess generator frequency above the participating lower edge. No smooth spectral density is required: the measure may contain atomic, absolutely continuous, or singular-continuous components. The integrated energy budget and the pointwise `hfR` relation are first-moment corollaries.

The manuscript establishes a second resource layer for **one fixed one-copy POVM**. Its complete retention sequence is positive definite, so the information retained at different temporal frequencies by one actual measurement cannot be chosen independently. This Herglotz/Toeplitz structure also extends across integer multiples of any chosen Bohr gap for an arbitrary semibounded pure-point Hamiltonian. Combining it with semibounded spectral tails yields

`Ebar+ >= hbar nu A(R(nu))`,

with `A(q) ~ [2(1-q)]^(-1/2)` as `q -> 1`. Therefore approaching unit retention at any nonzero gap requires divergent mean excess energy. A finite-chain sine-profile family under the canonical phase POVM realizes the same inverse-square-root scaling, proving that the divergence exponent is sharp; the globally optimal prefactor is not claimed.

A third result completely classifies exact one-copy saturators on the **full contiguous pure-sector chain**. First-harmonic equality is equivalent to the sector populations being a mixture of geometric distributions, equivalently to the survival sequence being a Hausdorff moment sequence. The same source-adapted POVM then saturates every harmonic simultaneously. Controlled continuum limits of exponential mixtures produce a completely monotone equality cone. This converse is deliberately not generalized to arbitrary anharmonic or sparse spectra.

These results connect quantum statistical inference, Bohr-frequency symmetry structure, semibounded energy, positive-definite functions, and classical moment theory. Established modes-of-asymmetry theory identifies which Bohr-frequency components are present; the theorem here instead bounds the **classical Fisher information extractable by any actual POVM** about perturbations of the random-time mixing law, with a population coefficient and energy-tail ceiling. Random-time dephasing, Herglotz/Bochner theory, and Hausdorff/Bernstein theory are explicitly treated as established ingredients rather than novelty claims.

For independent quantum-marked compound-Poisson sources, the modewise law is inherited under arbitrary parameter-independent source-to-bosonic-field dynamics and detector processing. Wavepacket overlap, propagation, loss, mode mixing, coherent detector memory, ancillas, and final joint readout can therefore be absorbed into the downstream channel. The manuscript also gives a coherent-sideband counterexample showing why baseline mean energy alone cannot constrain arbitrary parameter-dependent waveform-state synthesis.

The work is analytical. Six numerical scripts are used as adversarial consistency checks, including a deliberately incommensurate-spectrum test of the fixed-Hamiltonian Bohr-gap theorem. The separately optimized SLD-QFI comparison was removed from the main manuscript in favor of the more central fixed-Hamiltonian result.

We believe PRX Quantum is an appropriate first target because the manuscript now supplies a finite-copy operational resource ceiling beyond ideal harmonic spectra, a global consistency law for one physical measurement, a divergent near-lossless energetic cost with a sharp exponent, and a complete saturation classification in the model where such a converse is proved.
