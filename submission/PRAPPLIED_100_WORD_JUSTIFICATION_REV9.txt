Photodetectors are commonly compared using rise time, jitter, or electrical bandwidth, but these metrics do not directly quantify how much temporal information reaches the electrical record. This work derives the complete local weak-waveform Fisher-information transfer operator for autonomous photodetection event channels, exact detector-ordering and bandwidth results, and microscopic timing-resource bounds. It also provides closed-form mappings for canonical timing laws and a fit-free estimator of Fisher-equivalent bandwidth from impulse-response histograms, making the theory directly usable with existing detector data. Counterexamples identify when conventional timing metrics, synchronous references, and aggregate thermodynamic quantities are insufficient, without assuming a specific detector material or architecture.
