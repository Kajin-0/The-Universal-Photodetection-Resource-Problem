Photodetectors are commonly compared using rise time, jitter, or electrical bandwidth, but these metrics do not directly quantify how much temporal information reaches the electrical record. This work derives the complete local weak-waveform Fisher-information transfer operator for autonomous photodetection event channels, exact detector-ordering and bandwidth results, and microscopic timing-resource bounds. It also provides closed-form timing laws, a fit-free Fisher-bandwidth estimator, and a worked published-IRF example where FWHM and Fisher-equivalent bandwidth rank two real detectors oppositely. Counterexamples identify when conventional timing metrics, synchronous references, and aggregate thermodynamic quantities are insufficient, without assuming any specific detector material, architecture, or particular readout technology.
