Photodetectors are commonly compared using rise time, jitter, or electrical bandwidth, but these quantities do not directly quantify how much information about an incident optical waveform reaches the electrical record. This work derives an exact Fisher-information transfer operator for autonomous photodetection event channels and identifies the timing resources that bound its bandwidth. The results provide necessary-and-sufficient detector ordering for weak temporal waveforms, an exact Fisher-equivalent bandwidth, resource-cost inequalities, and counterexamples showing why familiar timing and thermodynamic summaries can be incomplete. The framework connects photodetector physics, information theory, and device-level bandwidth assessment without assuming a specific material platform or detector architecture.
